# Encounters as lesson plans

Greenrest was played by four players, one PC each (Gren, Jetten, Morgash, Thomas). Teaching a
D&D mechanic through play, rather than by explaining it, was a standing prep goal.

**Rule:** design each fight or arc to put **one mechanic** in the spotlight, cleanly learned.
Spectacle matters less than the lesson.

- Session 5's set is the model: the peryton road fight taught the **Ready action** against flyers
  (with thrown weapons as martial backup ranged); Thomas's Hollowing arc taught the **Exhaustion
  condition**; the same fight doubled as a low-stakes shakedown of the level-5 features (Extra
  Attack, 3rd-level slots).
- Teach trade-offs honestly: a readied attack is one attack, as a reaction — not Extra Attack — and
  readying versus repositioning is a real choice.
- Keep monster and rules complexity low and telegraph tactics.
- DM prompts at the table ("you could ready an attack…") are teaching, not hand-holding.

This is a standing design filter alongside keeping the plot simple.
