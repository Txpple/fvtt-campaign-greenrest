Appendix A

What If…?

The last session was written with six doors in it. The company opened one. These are the other five, as they were prepared — what each would have cost, and what it would have made of each of the four.

Appendix AWhat If…?

Both roads — hers and Shar's — needed all four shards at the tree, and every holder had a veto: Morgash held the Graveheart, Gren the Tideheart, Jetten the Duskheart, Invictus the Thornheart. Nobody could do either thing alone, and nobody could take a shard by force. Whatever the company chose, it had to be chosen by talking. That was the design, and it is why the argument on the lakeshore was the scene the whole campaign had been building toward.

A · Free her — the road taken

All four hearts into the knot, one at a time, each turning a quarter of the tree gold, with the town objecting in the square as it did. The Dreamer steps out, the crystal gives, and she goes. The Heart goes with her; nobody gets a resurrection. The leaves fall; the fog returns to the valley's rim; Enid's prayer is answered. This is the ending the company chose and the one the chronicle tells, and each of the four's fate is in the Epilogue.

B · Midnight into the knot

Shar's road. Invictus drives the black sword into the heart knot with the four shards at the tree; the blade goes in as if the wood had been waiting for it; the warmth goes out of the bark, out of the shards, and up the steel into his hand. The shards go grey and cold. The blossoms close. The tree stays green, and stops. When he pulls Midnight out it is heavier than it was, and it beats. The Dreamer is trapped under the square forever. The town, which heard her ask, chooses its summer anyway; nobody fights, nobody dies, and Oswin cries with relief and cannot look at the tree. There is a feast, a key to the town, and Invictus is named a friend of Greenrest.

Invictus. A month later, in Daggerford, five graves in a row. He draws Midnight and says their names one at a time, and the ground gives them back. Ann calls him Thomas, the way she always did; he can say all five of their names and still cannot say his own. One morning the youngest asks if he remembers the day he dropped the whole jug of syrup on the kitchen floor, and everyone laughs, and he laughs with them, and he does not remember it, and never will. There are a lot of days like that. But they are at his table, all five of them, and most days that is enough.

Morgash. Word travels — the dragon, the temple, the choice at the tree. Months later a shadow crosses the sun wherever he has ended up: a red dragon, and on its back, Lae'zel. I said I could not love anyone in this world. I have been at war since. I was wrong about you. You can make the tough choices and the sacrifices to save a people when they need to be made. There is a place at my side, my Morgash. I need you. Ride with me. Whether he goes is his to decide. The offer is real, and it is the one thing he wanted more than the chair by the fire.

Gren. The squirrels stop coming. Not one in the whole Wyrmwood will come down a tree for him now. Word reaches the warren and the elders send word back: don't come home. It is the second time they have cast him out, and this time the forest agrees with them; he sealed a fey creature into a tree for good, and nothing that lives under leaves forgets that. The Everseed Acorn went grey in his pocket the day the sword went in. He cannot plant it anywhere now.

Jetten. He does not come to the feast. By the time anyone thinks to look for him he is on the Trade Way going south, and he finds new company, the way elves do eventually. He does not write, and when someone mentions Thomas he changes the subject. The moonstone never glows again. His last words to Invictus, if there is a goodbye at all: You got five. She didn't get one. He means his sister.

Greenrest. The summer never turns. There is no autumn, ever again, and the children grow up without seeing a leaf fall. The town ties harvest ribbons around the scar in the knot. The dreams stop, and Greenrest sleeps well for the first time in a generation. Selma pours the tonic out, and nobody hears her anymore.

C · Walk away with the shards

The road that costs nothing and gives nothing. The shards can only be joined at the knot, so keeping them means keeping four separate stones, each with its own small power, and no resurrections for anyone. The company leaves Greenrest with the four hearts in their packs. The town keeps its summer. The tree keeps its buds and never quite blooms. She is not sealed, only left — and wherever they sleep, every night, she asks again. Someday someone else could carry the shards home.

Invictus. Carries the Thornheart and a sword he cannot use. The Duskheart's voice is quiet now — Shar called it her last offer and meant it — but the buried question is not: he crossed a forest for a name, found it, took it, and walked away from both the thing that could have brought his family back and the thing that could have set a stranger free. He has the pain again, undimmed. What he does with it is the whole of the rest of his life.

Morgash. Retires on the dragon's gems, as he always meant to, and finds the chair by the fire is not what he thought. The Graveheart on its cord under his shirt beats a little harder some nights than others, and he knows exactly what it is asking. He held a gate once against a netherbrain. This time he walked away from one.

Gren. The squirrels still come — he did nothing to the Green Lady, after all — but they ask about her, in the way squirrels ask, every time. The acorn stays green in his pocket and he never finds the place to plant it, because the one place that fit was the one he left with her still weeping under it. Pudgy follows him regardless.

Jetten. Sleeps in trance with the Duskheart on his chest and dreams her asking, every night, in his sister's cadence. He was the one who said it's not right to let her remain enslaved, and he was the one who could have made it happen by putting one stone in a knot. The moonstone glows every night, faintly, and he stops looking at it.

Greenrest. Unchanged, which is the point. Lucky, kind, comfortable, dreaming a little now that the tonic has failed, and never quite sure what four strangers took away with them in their packs.

D · The company cannot agree

No blades between friends, ever. Both roads need all four shards, so nobody can do either alone, and each holder decides what happens to their own stone. If even one holder will not give theirs up, neither road can be walked, and it ends as C — but the argument is the scene. The one the campaign was built for. On the morning it was played, the split was real: Invictus leaning Shar's way with the sword whispering, Jetten on record against, Gren and Morgash undeclared. It resolved on the lakeshore in about ten minutes of honest talk. Had it not:

Invictus holds the Thornheart, the only stone that can raise the dead on its own. He can keep his veto and keep a squirrel's worth of resurrection every dawn, and nothing else. The friends who held him back in the cell cannot hold his shard; they can only ask.

Morgash holds the Graveheart and has said out loud, twice, you will regret this. He is the one who told Oswin the honest version at breakfast. In a stalemate he is the one most likely to name the price plainly: a woman under the square, or a town above it, and which of us gets to decide for the other.

Gren holds the Tideheart and the standing that comes from being the one person in the company the forest trusts. If the split hardens, he is the one who can walk away from Greenrest and still be welcome under leaves. He is also the one who said if you do this, you will need redemption, and would have to decide whether to keep saying it.

Jetten holds the Duskheart, which is a veto and a temptation in one — see E. He is the company's conscience and Thomas's oldest friend, and in a true deadlock those two things pull opposite ways.

Greenrest waits. The tree keeps its buds. The elders meet. And every night four sleepers in the Long Rest are asked the same question by someone who cannot stop asking it.

E · Jetten uses the Duskheart on Invictus

Shar's own shard reaches into a memory once at dusk and changes it. The offer was less than a day old and lasted under ten minutes; Jetten could have erased it or rewritten it, and Invictus would have had one chance to resist. The elf who spoke Shar's words takes them back with Shar's own stone. If it works, Invictus forgets the offer entirely; Shar called it her last and meant it, and Midnight goes quiet. The company remembers what they did to him. He does not.

Invictus walks to the tree with a clear head he did not earn and a sword that has stopped talking. He frees her, probably, and it is a kindness — and the kindness is Shar's, delivered through his best friend's hands, at no cost to the goddess: the memory that hurts, taken. He is the campaign's proof that oblivion-as-mercy works. Somewhere he knows a piece is missing, the way he knew about his name.

Morgash would have argued against it — he is the one who wants Thomas to choose, and said so with his arms around the man's chest — and, outvoted, would have carried it. He does not forget things. He would look at Thomas at the tree and see a man who had been decided for, and think of the Basilisk Gate, and the nine who lived because someone else made the call.

Gren said Shar was the deepest of evils, and here is her shard doing her work on a friend. He would have wanted to be told it was necessary, and would not quite have believed it, and would have watched the acorn in his pocket for a week afterward to see whether it stayed green.

Jetten pays the price the shard was built to extract. He said it's the evil trying to work its way into you, and then used the evil's own instrument on the person he had known longest. The moonstone's last glow, in this ending, is not a gift from a grateful fey; it is Sehanine, lady of dreams, asking her devotee what he did to a man's memory.

Greenrest gets the same morning as A — the leaves, the fog, Enid's answered prayer — and never knows the difference. That is the whole trouble with it.

F · Raise Pudgy before they leave — the road taken

The Thornheart raises one body a dawn, dead ten days or less, and Invictus held it. Three squirrels lay in the great hall, one of them wearing Longshadow's feather; Honeysuckle and Pinenut would each have needed a dawn of their own, and it had to happen before any Shar's-road ending, since a drained shard raises no one. The company spent the very first raising on Pudgy, without being asked twice. Did we win? Where's Honeysuckle? They buried the other two under a tree with acorns, and a squirrel became a prophet. Of all the doors in the last session, this was the smallest, and it is the one that tells you most about who they were.
