The Broken Heart of Greenrest — Session 1

First Light on the Trade Way

July 7, 2026

Four seekers of the Broken Heart claimed their gear from the Silver Gauntlet's vault, marched south out of Daggerford, and found the Trade Way not as safe as its reputation: a slaughtered caravan on a bridge, a hobgoblin war band laired in the cave below, and — in the dead captain's hands — a rose-gold longsword that turned out to be waiting for one of them.

The Silver Gauntlet

Over last drinks at Daggerford's southern gatehouse, the company took each other's measure. Morgash the Gravemaker, orc veteran of the Flaming Fist, wants one last score and a quiet retirement — and swore vengeance at Thomas's side the moment he heard what happened to his family. Gren Greenmantle, gnome sorcerer of the Misty Forest, carries the map and the temple-ledger lore that started it all. Thomas A. Invictus, paladin of Lathander and one-time scribe, hunts the thieves who destroyed his household — a trail that first led him to whispers of the Broken Heart. And Jetten Elisedil, wood elf ranger and Thomas's friend of (his) lifetime, came to see him through it. The homework got done over drinks, too: Gren reached back into his childhood woods-lore and came up flawless — if a green dragon nests in the Misty Forest, it's young, or a wyrmling still growing into its cruelty, for no adult could stay rumorless in his home woods. And Thomas's old temple schooling surfaced with perfect, word-for-word clarity: a scholar's portrait of Shar, goddess of loss, night, and forgotten memories, whose faithful do everything in the dark. Then Lord Hargrove unlocked the tavern vault, and the party divided their stored arsenal — blades, wards, and a healthy pile of gold — before taking the south road.

The Road South

The Trade Way offered its parade of travellers. Two bickering "couriers," Pibb and Josk, asked the party to settle whether a horse needs a whole night's rest — and Thomas, who knows every honest document-dealer in Daggerford, read the man with a scribe's practiced eye — this was his own professional wheelhouse, and the tells were all there — and quietly marked Pibb for a forger, while Gren interviewed the horses themselves (well fed, well rested, no complaints beyond the indignity of being sat on). A barefoot halfling priest of Chauntea, Brother Tobin, blessed the company and pressed a barley token into Jetten's hand with a strange word about his goddess: "Some fields simply cannot fail; others starve a mile off. She doesn't usually play favorites." Of Greenrest itself, nobody on the road knew more than the name.

The Bridge and the Cave

Late in the day the road delivered its warning: a merchant cart overturned on a bridge, one wheel still spinning, crates looted, and a smear of fresh blood leading down to the riverbank. Morgash read a crowd of booted prints in the blood as easily as a page, and it was his ears — keener than the elf's, just this once — that caught the guttural laughter echoing up from below; the trail ended at the mouth of a lightless cave. Jetten slipped in ahead on elf eyes, quiet as falling ash, Gren tucked deeper still into the shadows behind him, and found them — a hobgoblin war band around a campfire, licking their wounds from the ambush above, a big captain among them. The debate was mercifully short. "We can't let them live," Thomas said. "They'll just attack whoever comes along the road next." Jetten drew his bow, marked his quarry, and loosed.

The Battle of the Cave

It was a proper brawl — captain, warriors, archers, and a shaman with a poison vine staff who came boiling up from the deeper dark mid-fight. Jetten's opening shot stung more than it wounded, but it bought the tempo: Thomas was fastest of anyone to steel, while Morgash's boots found every loose rock in the cave on his way in. The hobgoblins drew first blood regardless — an archer's arrow found a seam in Morgash's armor, deep and true, before he'd taken a step. Then the casters went to work: Thomas's Bane settled over a warrior and an archer — neither had the will to shake it — and curdled hobgoblin aim for the rest of the night, while Gren's Bless steadied the line. Morgash surged past his limits and, by sheer trained precision, willed a glancing stroke into a wound that mattered; when a warrior missed him back, he answered with cold riposte steel. Thomas's longsword bit the captain, and the smite behind it burned brighter than the cut; a breath later Jetten's arrow — loosed in the dark, across the full width of the cave — dropped the captain where he stood. Later came the blow of the night: Thomas's blade found the perfect seam, true as any stroke he's ever thrown, and he poured a doubled Divine Smite through the opening — the flash of holy light reduced an archer to very small pieces. Gren, meanwhile, conjured a false cave wall, ducked behind his own illusion, and popped out with scorching rays riding his innate sorcery: both rays flew perfect — twice critical — and the shaman burned where he stood. The vine staff had nearly returned the favor first: two poisoned blows beat Jetten to the ragged edge of standing ("I'm almost dead") and a warrior's longsword nearly finished the sentence — the same Jetten whose Healing Word had already pulled Morgash back from that edge minutes before. His answer was the truest arrow of the night, sunk to the fletching. When the dust settled, one hobgoblin stood alone: Morgash's pommel rang off his helm and Gren's roared "Surrender or die!" broke what nerve was left — the sword hit the floor. Asked who commands him, he looked at the corpse by the fire: "I take no orders from no one but my captain… I am captain now." His reign was brief. Jetten's shortsword ended it, a clean thrust to the eye: "Enough, hobgoblin scum."

First Light

The back of the cave held the hobgoblins' victims, barely an hour dead: two guards, a high-elf merchant woman whose fine dress Jetten's sharp eyes placed without a moment's doubt — a woman of means out of Waterdeep, a long way from home — and her companion. Thomas studied the guards' livery and knew it too well: paladins of Lathander, his own god. He read the psalm for the dead over them, taking nothing that was theirs, and something warm answered the prayer. And his knowledge of the road said the quiet part aloud: the Trade Way is not supposed to be like this — these hobgoblins were driven here from somewhere, scouring land they didn't know. The captain's blade was no hobgoblin steel: a rose-gold longsword that held a little daylight even in the dark, and over the night's rest it gave Thomas its name — First Light, a Dawnbringer's blade consecrated in a temple of Lathander. The party buried the dead with last rites, slept in the one clean corner of the cave, and woke to morning — and to the east, the treeline of the Misty Forest, exactly where the map says to leave the road.
