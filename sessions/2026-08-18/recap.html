The Broken Heart of Greenrest — Session 4

The Heart Under the Hill

August 18, 2026

They never went back to town. From the dead beasts' camp the party walked straight to the caves Marn's journal had warned them about — and found no shrine at all, but a catacomb, and five dead things that had been waiting five hundred years for company. Two of them wore Kestrel Company lockets. One of them wanted to die. And at the bottom, past a guardian with a burning scythe, sat the thing all of it had been built around.

Breaking Camp

The Kestrel Company's last camp made a decent staging ground, and the spoils got sorted properly at last. Gren claimed the Pearl of Power and handed the Periapt of Wound Closure back to the party stash to keep his attunements honest; Jetten took the quiver of silver-banded arrows, since he is the one with the bow; Morgash bound himself to the Maul of Momentum and pocketed the last two good healing potions. Then they spread out Sera's map of the four ruins and argued. Marn's journal had already told them what waited: "It is no shrine — it is a catacomb, cut deep under the hill, the dead laid in rows in the walls. And they did not stay in them." Morgash made the argument that carried — the Kestrels never got past the dead, so whatever they were looking for is probably still down there, and this time the party knows what is coming. Thomas noted the shrine was the farthest of the four from Greenrest, and said plainly that he liked it better than a hag coven. They slept at the camp, and in the morning they went southeast instead of home.

A Stair Cut for Nobody's God

The trail ended at a squat stone minaret pushing up out of the forest floor, with a staircase winding down into it. Gren did his due diligence first and spoke to a squirrel: "No, I don't go down there. That's not where food is. There are weird sounds, weird noises. We don't go down there." He fed it more rations than it could carry, asked it to come find him if it saw anything dangerous, and got nothing else for his trouble. He set a Light on the sun symbol of Thomas's shield, and they went down.

At the bottom: a figure carved from living rock, robed and taller than a man, worn past all recognition — no face, no hands. A shallow offering basin at its feet. And a skeleton on the floor, impaled on spikes that had been jammed into the stone long after the place was built; it fell, or was pushed, from the stair above. Thomas's scholarship gave the party more than they wanted. The work was centuries old, not decades — five hundred years without a scratch. It belonged to no faith he knew: no holy symbols, no scripture, no iconography of any living god. The chisel strokes were too small and too many for any tool still in use — the work of people with nothing but time. Whoever cut this was not following a tradition. They were making one up. And the offering basin had never once been used.

The Empty Coffins

Morgash opened a door and a skeleton came straight down the corridor at him. Jetten painted the crypt in Faerie Fire and set his Hunter's Mark. Morgash closed with an Adrenaline Rush; the skeleton swung, missed, and his Riposte shattered it across the flagstones. Thomas's javelin took the next one and left it staggering. Jetten's arrow found the archer at the far end — force and steel fused in the same instant, and the thing came apart in pieces. The maul finished the last.

Then they opened every coffin in the walls. All of them were empty. Gren said a small prayer over each one anyway. Jetten put it shorter: "Let the dead lie."

Aldous

The next door gave them zombies, an archer, and something upright and clearly dead standing in the middle of the room. It pointed a sword at them and said: "You will join us."

Gren opened his Innate Sorcery and put three Scorching Ray beams into it. The wight came on through Jetten's Entangle anyway and cut Morgash with a sword sheathed in cold green light. Then it reached out one hand — "Join me" — and something worse than a wound happened. The gash closed. What was underneath it did not. Morgash could feel the difference: not blood lost, but something taken out of him that he was not going to get back by breathing.

It happened again a moment later, and the party started fighting like people on a clock. Gren spent a Shatter nobody heard him cast — Subtle Spell, not a word or a gesture — and wrapped it in a Spellfire Burst. Morgash gave up on the maul, took up the greataxe, missed, and hauled the swing back on target with a Precision Attack, then Cleaved into the zombie beside it. Gren's Fire Bolt put one down for good: "Send it back to its grave." And Thomas ended it — First Light through the wight's chest, and it stopped moving.

What they found on the body changed the temperature of the whole night. Thomas's studies told him what a wight is — a corpse whose own wickedness keeps it walking — and then told him this one was wrong for the type. At its throat hung a bronze Kestrel Company locket. In its hand was a sword so cold that Gren refused to touch it, and wrapped it in cloth to carry it out. And Marn's journal, which Thomas read aloud again, had the name and the sentence both:

"Aldous's wounds closed, but he did not mend. Edda would not leave him."

The same words the party had just watched happen to Morgash. Thomas raised his holy symbol and said a prayer over the man, and the argument about resting ended quickly — nobody was going to sleep in a tomb with an orc who might not wake up as himself. Before they moved on, Gren showed them the acorn his village elders pressed into his hand the day they sent him away: taken from the heart-oak of his warren, still green after all these years, to be planted the day he finds where he belongs. "I still have not found the place where I belong."

The Keeper's Seat

A long pillared hall, a platform lost in the dark at the far end, and on it a wight seated on a throne who raised a hand and beckoned them in. Two more stood beside him. Skeletons filled the alcoves. He let them come, and he answered questions, which is more than the last one did. Asked what had become of Aldous's companion, he said: "She's here. She's with us. She's happier." Thomas asked the only question he had: "Why shouldn't we just destroy you here and now?"

The throne answered with the line of the night, and the parley was over.

"I thought you would be interesting. But your insolence irritates me. You will not make a suitable wight — you'll make an excellent zombie."

Jetten's first arrow was perfect — it took the archer through and dropped it before the line even formed. Then Edda raised a hand and Hold Person locked Jetten rigid where he stood. Morgash went at her. He missed; he dragged the swing back with a Precision Attack and connected hard enough to knock her flat, and she held her spell anyway. So he spent an Action Surge and hit her again, worse. That one broke it. Jetten came unstuck mid-stride.

Osric spent the fight taking Morgash apart from the inside. Every exchange, the same reaching hand; every time, another piece of him that did not come back. The orc kept standing. Thomas put Bane across the platform, bashed a skeleton off its feet with Shield Master, and worked First Light in with Sap so nothing that hit him hit him twice. And then Gren solved the room.

He opened the Driftglobe and chose daylight. The whole chamber went white. Osric and Edda shrieked and threw up their arms and shrank from it — and for the rest of the fight, nothing the dead swung came in straight. The skeletal mage was smart enough to answer: a sphere of Darkness dropped over the globe, smothering the daylight where it stood. Gren's reply was to walk over, pick up the globe — it weighs a pound — and carry the sun back out of the dark by hand.

After that it was arithmetic. Edda tried a Command on Morgash and he shrugged it off; his maul answered, the same weapon that had already broken her spell, and she went down beside the man she would not leave. Gren threw up Shield twice, once against the throne's own blade, Misty Stepped clear of the press, and emptied Magic Missile into whatever was still upright. Thomas called down Divine Favor and landed a strike so clean it lit the platform. Jetten got in close and worked Goldthorn and the shortsword in a Nick and Vex weave against a keeper who was, by then, flat on his back. Asked how he wanted to end it, Thomas did not hesitate: "I don't like this guy, so I'm going to chop his head off." The head went up, came down, and rolled all the way down the dais stairs.

Morgash crushed the last skeletal mage with the maul. On Edda's body: a second Kestrel locket. The throne itself held nothing at all. The east doors stood open; the west doors were locked.

Hesper, Who Was Tired

Through the east doors was another wight, flanked by two zombies, standing on a dais over a stone slab with chains on it. She waved the zombies still before anyone drew. She looked at the blood on them and worked it out for herself: "You came through the crypts. That means Osric is finally dead, or you would not be standing in that doorway." And then, almost kindly: "He was very old and very tired. I hope he was courteous with you. He usually managed courteous — at least, he used to. He doesn't suffer insolence well."

"I am Hesper. I will answer plainly. No need for riddles, no need for bargaining. To be honest, I haven't even talked to anyone in a very long time. What is your question?"

They asked, and she told them everything, in the flattest voice imaginable. She was the mortician. She prepared the wights. And five hundred years ago — she was not certain of the number and did not much care — she and Osric and Cadoc had been treasure hunters.

"We found a glowing shard in a cave. It was warm, beating, the size of two cupped hands. We saw it; we couldn't put it down. We brought it here to keep it safe. After time, our greed turned into undeath. We cut the stair so no one else could take it. We cut every way in that we knew. And so we stood with the Graveheart — lonely — but it gave us life. It's here, guarded by Cadoc, waiting for the next body to be prepared as a wight to keep us company."

That was the whole thing, laid out with no defence offered: not a curse, not a plot, just three friends who found something they could not put down and stayed with it until staying was all they were. The room the party was standing in was where new company got made. She said Cadoc would not talk to them the way she was talking — he was closest to the heart and furthest gone, and there would be no bargaining with him. Morgash asked whether she could undo what the drain had done to him. She could not.

Then she told them to take what was in her chest — a bone key for the locked west doors, and a stoppered flask, warm to the palm the way the shard is warm. "I have no use for either anymore. I'm sure you will need them both." And she asked.

"But I'm tired. I don't want to do this anymore."

Gren asked her how she would like it done. She walked to the slab where she had prepared so many others, and laid her head down on it. The party gave her what she asked for. Her body rolled off onto the floor, and in the same instant both zombies simply fell where they stood, as if someone had let go of a string.

There was one flask, and three men carrying holes in them. Morgash drank it, and everything that had been taken out of him settled back, and the cold went out. For Thomas and Gren the answer was less kind but at least it was an answer — Gren's schooling said the wounds close on their own after one full night's sleep in a real bed. Not in a dungeon.

The Guardian

Gren laid a Bless over all four of them and said what everyone was thinking: "Let's charge in. The clock is ticking." The bone key turned, the west doors came open, and the last room was waiting with its lights already on — a heap of dead piled up the far wall with red points burning in their eye sockets, an altar, and a hooded figure holding a scythe wrapped in fire that gave no heat. Behind him, mounted in a container, a great crystalline heart.

Cadoc moved first and he was fast. An arrow of cold green light at Morgash, and then, as Thomas crossed the floor, he simply opened a hand toward him from clear across the chamber. The dark between them caught like oil on water. Necrofire Reach. Thomas took the worst hollowing of the night from a man who never moved his feet.

Jetten's bow spoke first and clean. Morgash burned an Adrenaline Rush to reach him, missed, forced it home with a Precision Attack, and put him on his back with a Topple; then Action Surge, and hit him again while he was down. Thomas kept First Light on him with Sap so the scythe never came in true, then spent his last spell slot on a Divine Smite that burned all the brighter for what Cadoc was. Gren was down to scraps — a Fire Bolt, then Guiding Bolt, the second one landing and lighting him up for whoever swung next.

The skeletal mage kept fighting dirty to the end — darkness again, an ice knife, and finally a volley of Magic Missile that put Morgash on the floor. He did not stay there. Relentless Endurance caught him a hair above nothing. His Second Wind gave him almost nothing back, so he drank a potion instead and got up into the middle of five enemies who all wanted him specifically.

Cadoc swung the burning scythe at him and fumbled it badly. That was the end. Morgash's Riposte came back through the gap, and the maul followed it down, and it crushed his skull. The zombies and the skeletons took another moment and no attention at all.

What Jetten Saw

They stood in a room lit by its own dead, and the Graveheart sat in front of them: a great crystal heart, arterial red going to violet in the deep of it. Jetten reached out and took it. And it showed him something.

A beautiful green woman, glowing, white leaves crowning her head. She is crying. As she sobs she closes both hands over her chest — and her heart bursts. The pieces scatter into the dark, hardening as they fly. The burst drives the mists back all around her in a great ring, baring a green valley beside a beautiful lake. And where she stood: a tree. Gray. Lifeless. Asleep.

Then one faint heartbeat. And it was over.

Every one of them has seen that woman before, weeping on a hill in a night they were not supposed to dream. None of them has ever seen her do that. And not one of them needed to be told where there is a gray, lifeless tree standing in the middle of a valley beside a lake.

The first piece of the Broken Heart went into the party's keeping, and four very battered adventurers started for the surface.
