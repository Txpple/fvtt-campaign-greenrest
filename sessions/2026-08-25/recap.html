The Broken Heart of Greenrest — Session 5

The Long Rest

August 25, 2026

The party carried Thomas home to a town celebrating two miracles it credits them for — and spent three days pulling at Greenrest's seams: a tonic that stops dreams, a church whose goddess never answers, a dryad who calls them by a stranger's name, and a crystal heart that beats faster the closer it comes to the old gray tree. Then they chose their road — the Widow Fen — and something with a man's shadow and a stag's head tried to eat them on the way.

The Road Home

The tunnels were silent on the way out — the sobbing had stopped. Thomas could not so much as fasten his own pack, and by the last miles he could not walk unaided; Morgash cut him a cane from a straight branch buried in the brush. Jetten, working his cartographer's tools as they marched, drew the finest map of his life — every landmark inked with perfect, uncanny clarity — and the party made such good time that the forest never got a chance at them. Thomas had everyone watching the tree line for dead fields as they came down into the valley. There were none. Everything, everywhere, was in bloom.

Heroes Twice Over

Word of the displacer pack's end had reached town ahead of them, and Greenrest opened like a door. A scout walked them in beaming; Ambrose put hot food and ale in front of them on the house. A healer came to Thomas's sickbed that night with holly and herbs and a prayer to the Earth Mother — and nothing happened. Not a flicker. She looked bewildered, suggested the church infirmary, and hurried out. In a town this blessed, no prayer for healing has ever once been answered. The party noticed.

The Dream on the Hillside

That night, for the first time, they dreamed together — four people under two different roofs, standing on the same hillside, solid as daylight to one another:

  • Greenrest's valley below, but fuller — high summer laid on thick, and no wheat, no orchards. Just pure field and forest, as if the town had never come.
  • The young barefoot woman from the visions looked straight at them — through them — her face opening with a joy too large to look at: "Corin? …There you are. I knew you'd come back. Stay this time — stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever."
  • As she reached out, each of them felt a weight in their hands: the beating of the red stone Jetten carries, held in cold fingers that would not open.
  • Jetten's moonstone necklace burned bright green the whole time — again.

They woke in their beds at morning, all four at once. One real night in a real bed did what no field rest could: the hollow wound the wights left in three of them finally closed — the missing strength settling back like something returning home — and Thomas came back from the worst of it, gray and slow on his cane, but climbing.

Three Days in Greenrest

The party worked the town like investigators. Thomas played sicker than he was to slip upstairs and cast Detect Poison and Disease over the Greenrest Tonic and even the free breakfast: clean, both. Ambrose, asked about the tonic, shrugged — everyone takes it, weekly, since childhood, or else "this dead dryad keeps haunting our dreams." At the apothecary, Selma showed her wares and her recipe without blinking — smashed berries, strained apple, a secret herb from her own garden, taught to her by the apothecary before her — and Gren, reading her under a whispered Guidance, found her honest to the bone. It was Selma who opened one of the Kestrel lockets the party carried and found what they had missed: a letter, folded small, never sent. Marn to his Tess — four old places in the forest, a prize in exactly one of them, "the shrine wasn't a shrine. A graveyard" — and a promise to come home and buy the house by the tannery. The one she liked.

Elsewhere: Gully the blacksmith took one look at Brakk's claw-rent plate and swore he'd work all night — payment taken in trade, two of the party's grim necrotic swords, which he means to study. Mother Wend bought the rest of the Kestrels' arms and told the town's story over the counter — a fogless valley, endless summer, fruit four times a year, two years' militia service for everyone, and no elf, gnome, or dwarf who ever chose to stay. At the church, Thomas — a Lathanderite with a scribe's eye for stonework — read the building itself with dawn-lit clarity: even the oldest carvings are fruits and farming. Whoever raised this grand stone pile built it for the Earth Mother — and completely misunderstood her. Enid confessed what the party had already witnessed: the goddess has never once answered her prayers. "I didn't choose this," she said of the gilded basement. She's starting to wonder too.

Coll the gravedigger — the town's one open malcontent — admitted he sometimes skips his tonic just because he hates taking medicine, dreams the dreams, and goes back on it. Gren asked him, next time he skips it, to come talk. And little Pip, between rounds of bragging and a new card game, dropped the sharpest clue of the day without knowing it: Selma, alone in a town celebrating, is not happy the tree is changing.

Mayor Oswin received them like family and gave them the town's inheritance of secrets: he and Selma both privately believe the endless summer flows from the dryad, not the goddess — "that cleric has never cast a real divine spell in my life." Shown the Kestrels' charcoal sketch of a weeping woman, he knew her at once from his own childhood dreams: that's her. And then the butler brought the news that bent the whole town's day: the old gray tree at the heart of Greenrest, barren for every generation anyone can name, had put out its first green buds that very morning. Standing under it afterward, Jetten made a quiet test: the closer he carried the hidden Graveheart to the tree, the harder it beat — and it faded again with every step away.

The Second Dream

The next night the hillside came again, word for word — "Corrin? Stay this time" — and this time, as she reached out, Jetten answered. He held out the heart. Her eyes widened — and the dream ended, cut clean, like a held breath. In the morning Thomas stood up whole, handed back his cane, and the party started packing.

Longshadow

The council picked the Widow Fen — the hag coven's ground — and saved the dragon for a stronger day, each of them pocketing one of Selma's antitoxins against that future. The forest had other plans for the afternoon. Jetten caught it first: a man's shadow sliding over the grass, cast by nothing a second look could find. The squirrels, once Gren asked them properly through Speak with Animals, were blunt: Longshadow. Flying stag. Feeds on people. Better get running.

Three of them came out of the sky over the ruin clearing. Jetten struck first — Hunter's Mark, an arrow driven home, his Dread Ambusher training making the opening shot a cruel one. Gren swore a free-spoken Bless over the front line. Then the flock chose its meals. Longshadow dove on Jetten again and again, horns and talons, battering the mark out of his mind twice over. A peryton broke itself on Thomas's brand-new plate — strike after strike turned by dawn-forged steel. Thomas answered with First Light, sapped the beast's nerve mid-swing, and slammed it flat to the ground with his shield — Shield Master, textbook. Gren unleashed Innate Sorcery and a Scorching Ray that found two perfect seams — and somehow still barely singed feathers, the kind of luck that gets a sorcerer heckled for a week.

Then Morgash ended the argument. Longshadow dropped low for its kill and met the Maul of Momentum coming the other way. One swing. The terror of this stretch of forest hit the ground and did not move. Next round he took the greataxe to the grounded peryton — hit, Cleave into its wingmate, willed a miss into a hit with a Precision Attack, then Action Surge and another axe-fall that killed it outright, the follow-through of his Great Weapon Master's hew still hungry for more. Thomas finished the last one with First Light as it flailed at his armor. Three man-shadowed hunters down; not one of the four seriously hurt.

Gren plucked a fistful of Longshadow's feathers, pressed one into a squirrel's paws, and commissioned the forest itself as his herald: "Tell all the beasts that Gren the Mighty has slain Longshadow!" A minute later, six squirrels returned and solemnly spat six acorns at his feet. Tribute.

Spoils & Progress

  • Brakk's plate armor, reforged by Gully — won (loosely speaking) by Thomas after a roll-off for the ages. He now stands behind dwarven plate, a raised shield, and a warded cloak — very little in this forest can touch him.

  • The Graveheart & the Periapt of Wound Closure — claimed by Morgash to attune. If anyone falls, the heart can call them back — once it knows him.
