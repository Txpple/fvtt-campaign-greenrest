The Broken Heart of Greenrest — Session 2

The Lure in the Mist

July 14, 2026

East off the Trade Way and into the Misty Forest: a blight ambush at a quiet clearing, a false light that led to an ettercap lair in a ruined elven temple, a fey scimitar with a name of its own — and, from the last rise, Greenrest at last: a perfect green village with one gray, dying tree at its heart.

Into the Misty Forest

Morning found the party rested at the cave and short on answers. Thomas put the question to the one local at the table — and Gren, raised in these very woods, had never heard of Greenrest at all. The Misty Forest keeps its settlements small, secluded, and private; a village unknown even to a forest gnome is no strange thing here. Morgash dragged the wrecked cart off the road, and the company turned east into the trees. Jetten scouted ahead through country that felt like home, Gren walked land he'd known all his life — and yet, hour after hour, it was Morgash, the Baldur's Gate city orc on his first real woodland march, who kept picking the right trail while the ranger and the native second-guessed themselves. Nobody could explain it, including Morgash.

A Rest, Interrupted

Past midday they stopped to rest in a clearing — and the forest went quiet around them. The birdsong stopped; the mist stood still. Jetten's instincts and Gren's woods-lore both rang the alarm in time, so when the underbrush stood up — five blights of twig, thorn, and coiled creeper, ringing the camp — nobody was caught flat-footed. Jetten was fastest: his opening arrow dropped a vine blight before it ever took its turn. Morgash halved a twig-thing with a single stroke, then found a burst of adrenaline and crossed the clearing to block the far side of the ring. Thomas fought methodically — every hit from First Light left its target flinching and clumsy — and when grasping vines erupted around his boots he stepped out of them, laughing: "You will never be able to catch me." Gren picked fire for his spellfire on a hunch, exactly the right call against dry wood. The last blight fell to a perfect stroke of First Light, and the quiet returned — not one of them had landed a blow. Gren said what everyone was thinking: these things are a corruption of life itself, and somewhere out there is a source.

The Light That Beckoned

Hours later the trees gave way to bare wet rock: a low gray cliff, split at its base by a cleft taller than a man. Before the opening hung a single small light — round, warm, candle-like, and wrong in a way that took a moment to name: it cast no glow on the stone, and it threw no shadow. It drifted toward them and back again, insistent, almost pleading. Gren's soft heart answered first — one of our fairy friends may be in distress — and the party followed it into the dark: cold air, wet stone, and something sweetly rotten underneath. Gren set his light cantrip on night-blind Thomas (a tradition now), and Jetten crept ahead — where his sharp eyes picked the ambush out of the webs before it could spring. "How delightful. It was a trap, guys." The friendly little light winked out.

The Web-Halls

What waited below was a brood of ettercaps — and the night's best joke arrived right on cue: Morgash, all-day navigator of the Misty Forest, froze at the first sight of trouble and went dead last into a fight he was standing at the front of. But he made the turn count when it came: the first ettercap kept stealing nervous glances at a closed door, and Morgash read it — he dashed across the hall and planted himself squarely in front of that door before anything could come through it. Jetten opened blade-in-each-hand and carved into the first ettercap; Thomas laid a blessing over the line that quietly paid off all fight. The wounded creature threw back its head and wailed — and the deeper halls answered. A thrown web strand stuck Thomas fast where he stood. The door Morgash was guarding burst open under the Broodmother, half again the size of her children, and her bite put venom in his blood — but she got no further than the doorway he was holding. More broodlings came in along the walls and ceiling — and the friendly light returned, hostile now: a will-o'-wisp, striking with lightning out of thin air and vanishing again before any blade could answer. Gren burned the first ettercap down with scorching rays — two of them flew perfectly — then turned his silent, unerring magic missiles on the Broodmother. Thomas sawed himself free of the webbing, kept his friends on their feet with healing hands, and killed a broodling outright with a perfect stroke ("We're going to call you Thomas the Silent"). Morgash, twice poisoned at his post, cured himself twice from his jar of field ointment and kept turning misses into hits by trained precision. Jetten's arrows wore the Broodmother down — and Gren's volley of magic missiles finished her. The will-o'-wisp escaped: it vanished after its last attack and never came back. Gren has not forgiven it.

The Hidden Shrine

With the webs burned away, the lair showed what it really was: a ruined temple — worked stone, worn statues, etchings too old to read. Thomas's temple schooling placed it: a shrine of the fey, elven most likely, its bowls made for alms and water, its halls built small the way elves build. Gren, without a word, poured clean water into each empty bowl before they left. The bones scattered through the halls were picked clean. Down a south passage lay a more recent victim — a fey figure, months dead, a dagger still at its side — beside the hoard of everything the lights had lured in over the years. From it the party took a cloak woven for silence, a hooded lantern whose light strips the invisible bare, a healing draught, a purse of gold — and two stranger things. The first: a gilded scimitar that wanted to speak to Jetten, the same quiet pull Thomas once felt from First Light. Attuned overnight, it gave up its name — Goldthorn, lost with its bearer to the webs of a hidden temple in the Misty Forest, waiting on a worthy hand, carrying one bloom of clinging faerie light between dawns so that nothing may hide in shadow. The second was smaller and stranger: a Dose of Greenrest Tonic — a stoppered clay vial of bitter green cordial, found among the web-wrapped effects of a traveller who never left the temple. The label, in a tidy, careful hand, reads only: "One swallow, seventh-day, as ever. — Selma." Thomas also spoke aloud, to a silent room, what they learned of it:

"Whoever drinks the swallow sleeps utterly, without dreams, for the next seven nights. No apothecary could name any other virtue… nor say why anyone would brew such a thing."

The village's name found them before the village did. They slept in the one clean corner of the shrine they had cleared — Jetten trancing on watch by the entrance, new sword close at hand.

First Sight of Greenrest

In the morning the trail climbed one last rise, and the forest let them go — the mist simply ended in cold, clean sunlight. Below, in green meadows under a cloudless sky, lay the village: slate roofs snug around a square, chimney smoke rising straight, orchards and hedged fields, birdsong, cut hay, woodsmoke. An old stone watchtower keeps the height nearby, a worn path spilling from its foot. Greenrest, at last — looking like the reward at the end of a long road. Only one thing catches the eye: in the very heart of the village, alone in all that green, a single great tree gone gray and sickly. The one wrong note in an otherwise perfect song.
