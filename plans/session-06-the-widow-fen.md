# Session 6 — "The Widow Fen"

_The Broken Heart of Greenrest · Status: played 2026-08-31_

**This shard is MORGASH'S SPOTLIGHT.** The fen is to Morgash what the Temple of Shar is to Thomas:
the site that reads his grief back to him and prices it. Every other thread in the session bends
around that.

## 0 · The shape of the night

> **The Widow Fen** — two grieving sisters keep house over a drowning bog, their dead sister's
> chair still set at the cauldron. The party fights its way to their door, is received like
> long-expected guests, trades at their table — and learns the Tideheart can only be taken the way
> it can't be bought: over the sisters' objection, in their own home.

1. **The road out** — cold open where session 5 wrapped: Longshadow dead at the Temple Ruins.
   March northeast, the tithing-stone on the moor path, the land drowning by degrees.
2. **The swamp guards** *(01 Swamp)* — fog, root-islets, and the opposed-perception opener.
   The bullywug guards **attack on sight — no parley**.
3. **The house** *(02 Treetop)* — the flavor romp: a few more bullywug guards in ones and twos,
   cabins guarded by fey-enchanted animated furniture that fights room by room, and lore
   scattered throughout: the Daggerford parcels, the sisters' grief-trade, the slayer's face,
   two easter eggs. No timer — it runs until they find the trunk stair going DOWN.
4. **The parlor** *(03 Cavern)* — tea by the cauldron. The four-beat Ethel/Morgash recognition:
   delight → freeze → Lae'zel's voice → the toast.
5. **The table** — truth sold under the metering rules; Thomas reads the jar-wall; the shard-tax
   recitation and the *"one chamber — a heart has four"* countdown land here; then the two offers
   go down: **the partnership** (gather the Heart together — one beat wakes their sister, the
   other three are the party's), and the offer to **cut Morgash's heartbreak out**. Neither
   resolves tonight; both stand.
6. **The break** *(flex)* — they make the offer; if the party doesn't take it, this likely ends
   in a fight to the death: hags attuned to the Tideheart, the household
   converging down the trunk.
7. **Homecoming** *(almost certainly session 7)* — reveal dream A.2 at two shards, dressed in the
   fen's trace; the tree buds. Do not rush the table to reach it; the parley is the session.

## 1 · Open — the road out, and the land drowning

- Cold open at the **Temple Ruins** clearing, exactly where the table stood up: Longshadow and the
  peryton cooling in the grass, Gren's proclamation to the beasts of the forest still hanging in
  the air. One beat of aftermath (harvesting, the squirrel herald if it recurs), then move.
- **The march northeast, several hours, montaged in three cuts** — the land changes under them:
  1. Forest thinning, ground going soft; boots start coming up with a sucking sound.
  2. **The tithing-stone** on the moor path — a waist-high standing stone, its top worn into a
     shallow bowl, old wax and twine scraps in the moss around it. This is §5's Daggerford
     drop-point, **seen before it's understood** — a cart could stop here, leave a parcel, and
     never see who collects. Let them inspect it; explain nothing. (Payoff lands in §5/§6.)
  3. Open fen: black water, root-tangles, and a mist that doesn't burn off at noon.
- **First sight of the house** — read-aloud, at the fen's edge:

> **READ-ALOUD — the fen's edge.** The forest gives up before you do. The ground ahead is black
> water broken by islets of dead root-tangle, and the mist lies on it in banks, moving without any
> wind you can feel. Far out — you can't tell how far; the fog keeps changing its mind — one
> enormous tree stands up out of the swamp, wide as a keep tower, and there are **lights in it**.
> Cabins. Rope bridges. Somebody built a house in the crown of that tree, and somebody is home.
> Between you and it, the water is perfectly still. Which is strange, because the reeds keep
> moving.

- **Road logistics:** nights on the road are dreamless (ledger rule — the contrast is the clue).
  If they camped between Longshadow and the fen, one line: nobody dreamed. The party is **level 5**
  (verify the three pending level-ups landed — checklist).

## 2 · The swamp guards — 01 Swamp, and who sees whom first

**Scene:** *01 Swamp*. On the map: fog
overlays live across the whole board; six-plus root-islets with cut-stump platforms; boat-docks at
the great tree's southern base **with a moored boat**; a second dock on the southwest islet; a
**drowned corpse on the rocks bottom-center** (free prop — a drowned trespasser, picked clean, pockets
turned out); a wrecked boat on the southeast islet. The great tree's trunk-stair entrance sits at its base (it leads DOWN — the ladders lead up).
**The guard tokens are already placed and hidden**: the Bog Sage enthroned on the north-center
islet, warriors north, west, and two south by the docks.

**The opener — the perception contest** ("who sees who first," kept simple,
taught out loud after it resolves):

- The bullywugs are **guards, and they attack on sight — no parley, no toll**. They are **not hiding from the party; they live
  hidden** — squatting in reed-clumps and neck-deep water around the islets, watching every
  approach.
- **One roll each side.** The gang rolls **one group Dexterity (Stealth) check with advantage**
  (Swamp Camouflage). Each PC rolls **Wisdom (Perception)** — sight is fog-fouled out here, so
  a PC relying on eyes alone takes **disadvantage beyond 30 ft**; call out that listening and
  watching the water is what works (croaks carry; still water ripples). **Jetten's ranger eyes are
  the spotlight** — let him be the one who finds the pattern in the reeds.
- **Whoever wins, sees first:**
  - **Party spots them** → the ambush is blown; the guards come up out of the water screaming.
    Party acts on clean initiative; **guards roll initiative at disadvantage** (2024 surprise
    rule, taught by name).
  - **Guards spot them** → the first croak comes from **behind**: warriors already flanking them
    from the water on both sides. **Party rolls initiative at disadvantage**, and round-1
    attacks from bullywugs still unseen in the reeds have advantage until they're located.

**The fight** (~1,700 XP, Low-to-Moderate for four at level 5):

- **1× Bullywug Bog Sage** (CR 4) — the herald, fights from his islet throne, spells over spear.
- **4× Bullywug Warrior** — their whole doctrine is the **shove**: knock PCs off the islets
  into black water, then pile on the swimmer. Black water is the hazard the map is made of.
- **2× Giant Toad** (CR 1) — the drowning clock, taught in round two: a swallowed PC is being
  carried *under*. Make the water the villain.
*(No crocodile — Old Mother Gulla is out of the encounter — and no scripted alarm-runner; the
fight simply ends where it ends.)*

the central study, a rug of smothering in the northeast cabin, sword + rug in the south cabin,
broom + rug in the southwest bunk-cabin — and **bullywug house-guards on the platforms**, with the

- **Getting up:** **two ladders** climb from the swamp to the crown — west and east (prod regions

## 4 · The parlor — the Ethel reveal, and Morgash

**Scene:** *03 Cavern*, reached only by descending inside the trunk. On the map: the stair lands at the root-hub dead center; candle-glow
lighting is already placed and the cavern reads properly dark. The furniture carries the plan —
**jar-shelves and crates along the east wall** (the larder's big sibling — §5's cipher wall), **two
cages and two corpse slabs southwest** (the trade made visible), a **great beast's skull and
ribcage northeast**, a **pale huddle in the north alcove** (dress to taste — *not* undead; the
Hollow owns this campaign's undead), the **tea-table** center-south by the cauldron — **three
chairs. One is empty, and it is the best chair.** The **northwest stone stair** is the bolt-hole,
masked by bushes and illusion up top (§6).

**The Masie gambit** (staging: Masie visible/neutral in
the cage area, **Mabel's token invisible beside her**, Hazel and the animated set hidden). The
cavern the party walks into is **empty except for a child**: "**Masie**," maybe eight, crying in
one of the cages, begging for help — she says she was **kidnapped from Daggerford**. She is **an
illusion** — **Mabel stands invisible just outside the cage**, doing the voice,
working the party before the parley they don't know is already happening:

- **What she's doing:** pumping them for everything the sisters want to know before tea — who
  they are, what they carry (*is it the red stone? can I see it?*), who's strongest, who's
  kindest, who lost someone. Every answer they give "Masie" comes back at the tea-table, quoted.
- **The reveal, on her terms or theirs:** when she has what she wants — or the moment she's
  caught — "Masie" snuffs out like a pinched candle, and **Mabel drops her invisibility just
  outside the cage**, cackling, while **Hazel comes out of the dark** behind them. The delight beat
  (below) begins with the hags *already briefed*. If the party spilled to Masie, beats 1–2 land
  harder: the freeze on Morgash can quote something he said to the "child."
- **Tone guard:** this is a head-game, not a horror bait-and-switch on the players — reward the
  ones who were kind to Masie (the hags noticed; kindness reads as *quality grief* and improves
  the trading mood) and the ones who caught her (professional respect).

**The sisters:** **Hazel** — elder, the danger, the brewer; **Mabel** — younger, merchant and
mimic, pours the tea and does the voice. Dead sister completes the "-el" set: *Ethel, Hazel, Mabel.* They say only
**"our sister"** through the whole parley — the "ETHEL" name-drop is just an easter egg (it lands
at the freeze); don't over-emphasize it.

Play the recognition as a four-beat swing:

1. **Delight.** Hags read grief professionally; four walking griefs enter their bog. They circle,
   sniff, coo over the merchandise.
2. **The freeze.** One hag stops dead at Morgash. She tastes something on him — githyanki love,
   rare vintage. Then she *places* it. The temperature drops instantly: *"He stinks of the
   sword-maid. He **loves** the one who slew our ETHEL."* Real danger spike — this is the closest
   the scene comes to combat before any bargaining.
3. **The inspection.** Before blades come out, the other sister actually *looks* into him — and
   what she finds turns fury into astonished, cackling glee: Lae'zel hollowed him out worse than
   she hollowed the coven. Have her quote the goodbye **verbatim, in Lae'zel's voice** (Mabel;
   Mimicry is the license): *"I cannot love someone in this world… goodbye, my Morgash."* This is
   a huge table beat for free — Anthony deliberately withheld Lae'zel from the dream-sharing
   (session-5 GM notes flag the asymmetry), and the hags just outed his heartbreak in front of the
   party.
4. **The toast.** *"Our sister is avenged already, dearie. Look what the slayer did to him — she
   does our work for us wherever she walks."* They decide they *like* him. Which is worse than the
   fury.

**How they know:** when Ethel died near Baldur's Gate, the surviving two *felt* it through the
coven bond and scried the death — they know the killer's face, and it's Lae'zel's. (The library
sketches are that scrying, drawn and redrawn — if the party found them, this is the click.)

**The Morgash bargain** (his mirror of Thomas's Shar temptation): they offer to **cut the
heartbreak out of him** — bottle it, take it as payment, he walks out light as a boy. The cost,
stated honestly: the love goes with it; you cannot keep the summer and sell the winter. That is
Corin's line — *"if you take the ending out of me, there's nothing left that you love"* — wearing
a hag's face, and it's Selma's tonic writ personal: a grief *removed* instead of *finished*, which
is the whole campaign thesis. Run it under the Shar-temptation rules (plot doc §6.3): no Insight
DC dissolves it, don't resolve it, the offer stands all campaign.

## 5 · The table — what's for sale, and what it costs

Guests at a widow's table, not customers at a counter. Over tea:

- **Thomas and the jar-wall.** The east shelves are the larder writ large, every label in the
  ring's cipher. The hags clock his face reading them and are *delighted*: "Oh — a customer's
  customer!" They answer freely — no shame, nothing to hide. **What they know:** prices,
  drop-points (the tithing-stone the party already passed; a hired cart, driver paid blind), the
  quality — *"mourner's teeth, widow's tears, a mother's little finger — grief seasons the meat,
  dearie, Daggerford grief was always the best."* **What they don't, and this is the wall:** *"We
  never met a face, poppet. Meat doesn't need introductions."* No names, no destinations, nothing
  about the murders. *(The Shar temple is unrelated to the Daggerford thread —
  there is nothing to signal toward.)* The ache detail, unprompted: *"the
  Daggerford man stopped sending, oh, half a year gone."* Thomas does the date math himself.
  **Hard guards:** nothing of his family is ever in those jars. His dead-count is **five** (wife
  Ann + four children, per his backstory journal).
- **The truth, metered.** A good merchant never dumps inventory. At one shard they sell
  **identity and anatomy, never biography**: what the shards are — chambers of one crystal heart —
  never Corin's story, never the valley's luck. Tier-3/4 material is *on the shelf and priced out
  of reach*, named tantalizingly and refused cheerfully, which books the return visit right into
  the two-going-on-three window. Line-by-line gets winged at the table; the owed beats that MUST
  land tonight:
  - **The shard-tax recitation** (owed since session 5): crystal · obviously still a heart · warm
    · it beats · never takes a mark.
  - **The countdown:** *"one chamber — a heart has four."* Still unspoken at the table; a hag
    saying it over tea is the cleanest delivery it will ever get.
- **Why they want the shards** (the lore dump they'll give free, because it's a sales pitch): the
  whole Heart, joined, holds **four castings of true resurrection — one per chamber**. Two hags
  are a broken instrument — hags are most powerful in three, and they'll say so bitterly. Their
  plan: assemble the Heart, spend one beat of it on their sister, be a coven again. *(They are a
  perfect dark mirror of the finale's keep-the-Heart option — a family of two grieving their dead
  third, planning to keep a weeping heart in a cage to get her back. The party is looking at
  their own temptation and doesn't know it.)*
- **The partnership offer** (the hags do NOT buy or sell shards): they want partners, not customers. *"We don't want your stone — we want your
  legs. Help us find the green and the black. When the Heart beats whole: **one beat wakes our
  sister. The other three are yours. Anyone you like.**"* Then watch the table. Thomas and
  Jetten's eyes are your first finale read. The three-for-one split says the arithmetic of four
  out loud — that's intended; what stays unspoken is whose keeping the whole Heart ends up
  in, and what the Dreamer thinks of any of it.
- **And they will not sell the Tideheart.** Their sister's chair, their stake in the Heart, the
  third member of the coven (§6). Every price the party names is met with the same answer: *"It's
  not ours to sell, dearie. It's HERS."* — a glance at the empty chair.

## 6 · The break — the cavern fight ⟨flex — may slide whole into session 7⟩

They make the offer. If the party takes it — or withdraws (the hags will let them walk, smiling:
*"do come back when you've lost something"*) — the session ends on the walk out. Otherwise this
likely ends in a **fight to the death**:

- **The hags:** both MM Green Hag chassis buffed to **CR 5** (PB +3,
  **HP 132** each). Slotless free casts: **Misty Step at will** (bonus) on both; **Hex 1/day**
  (bonus) on both; **Hazel** adds Suggestion 1/day + Hellish Rebuke 1/day (reaction); **Mabel**
  adds Dissonant Whispers 1/day + Mirror Image 1/day.
- **The Tideheart is the third sister** — seated at the cauldron, the shard keeps **Coven Magic** (and their scrying) online
  with only two hags. **One of the hags carries it in the fight — that's the whole
  kit**. **Taking the shard from her (or her death) audibly breaks the coven** —
  that beat is firm.
- **The household converges:** every surviving bullywug and every animated set comes down the
  trunk over rounds 2–4 — the fight gets worse on a clock the party can hear (croaking, and then
  furniture on the stairs).

**The Tideheart:** *deep water
blue, like light rising through black water. The chamber the blood comes home to.* It mirrors the
Graveheart exactly — same structure, same vision, no attunement:

- **The vision** — identical to the Graveheart's first-hold vision (the heart-burst), verbatim.
- **While you carry it:** advantage on **Strength saving throws** (Active Effect, transfer:
  `str.save.roll.mode +1`, matching the Graveheart's CHA effect).
- **Once between dawns:** casts ***death ward*** (linked PHB compendium cast activity consuming
  1 item use, 1/dawn recovery — the Graveheart's revivify pattern; flavored as cold salt water
  flooding the lungs and receding).
- Item uses 1/dawn · legendary · trinket · magical.
