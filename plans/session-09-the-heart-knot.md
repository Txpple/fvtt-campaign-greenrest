# Session 9 — The Heart Knot

The last session · Shar's offer · the fourth dream · the tree · the choice. Status: played 2026-09-29.

## PART ONE · SHAR'S OFFER

Open the session on it. They are still in the Wyrmwood: the dragon is dead, the squirrels are dead, and the four hearts are all in the room at once for the first time.

**The Duskheart is on Jetten, so Andrew reads Shar's words.** The elf who cut Harrow in half with Goldthorn is the one who says it's forgiven, and the one who told Invictus it's wrong to leave her enslaved is the one who asks him to do it.

> **NARRATE (to Jetten).** The Duskheart has gone cold against your chest. It has never been cold before. Your hand closes on it without you deciding to. You open your mouth, and the voice is yours, but the words aren't.

Show Andrew the card (a journal page shown only to him). He reads it aloud to Invictus, cold, for the first time.

> **THE DUSKHEART SPEAKS**
>
> *The Duskheart has gone cold against you. You aren't in control of what you say. Read this aloud to Invictus, slowly, and add nothing.*
>
> Invictus.
>
> Killing my high priest was regrettable. But it is forgivable.
>
> My offer still stands.
>
> Take the heart shards and Midnight to the Dreamer's tree. Find the tree's heart knot, and plunge the blade into it.
>
> Midnight will drain the power from the tree and the shards. The Dreamer will be trapped. Each shard can grant you a resurrection, and the heart of the tree will grant you a fifth. The shards will be dead, but Midnight will have the ability to give you your five resurrections.
>
> You will win. You will get your family back.
>
> The town will win. The Dreamer's summer will remain in Greenrest forever.
>
> I will win. My will shall be done.
>
> Think carefully, Invictus. This is my last offer.

No save, no roll. It lasts as long as the speech, and Jetten remembers every word. When it's over, the Duskheart is warm again. Then let the table sit in it. Don't prompt Invictus.

## PART TWO · THE FOURTH DREAM

The first night back in a Greenrest bed. The whole town dreams this one, and this time everyone can see everyone.

> **NARRATE.** No hillside this time. You stand in what's left of the memory: the bare hill, the fog close, the tree where she stood. You aren't alone. The whole town is here with you, in their nightclothes, and there's no mist between you anymore. Oswin, Selma, Enid, Ambrose, Warin, Pip: every face you pass in the square, as clear as you are to each other. And for the first time in four dreams, she is looking at you.

> **THE DREAMER:**
>
> "You've been here before. In the summer, at the fog, at the breaking. You're the ones holding my heart."
>
> "I forgive him. Corin chose his ending, and he was right to. I forgive him for leaving."
>
> "And I forgive myself. For holding on so long. For all of it."
>
> "So I'm asking you. Free me."
>
> "Bring the hearts to my tree. Find the heart knot. Place them in it, one by one, and I will absorb them. On the fourth, I will be free."
>
> "Please. Help me. I have been trapped for centuries."

Before she looks at Invictus, she looks at Midnight.

They wake, and the whole town wakes with them, at the same moment. All four hearts are beating in time. Cut there.

## PART THREE · THE HEART KNOT

Both speeches name the same spot. Whatever the party decides, it gets decided here.

> **NARRATE.** The tree in the square has more blossoms than yesterday, and half the town has come out to look at it. Nobody is talking about the dream, and everybody had it. At chest height on the trunk there's a knot the size of two cupped hands, shaped like a closed heart. The bark there is warm. Put your palm on it and wait, and it beats.

| Who | Where | Doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enid | Kneeling at the knot | Kept a vigil all night. Believes the Earthmother sent the dreams. Won't move for anyone. |
| Warin | Two men on every side of the tree | "My men all woke at the same time. I've got them on the tree, double." |
| The children | Around the trunk | Playing The Breaking in their red, green, blue and purple shirts. |
| Oswin | Coming to find the party | What he says depends on what they mean to do (A or B). |

| Heart | Holder | Power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graveheart (red) | Morgash | 1/dawn, returns a creature dead under a minute (revivify) |
| Tideheart (blue) | Gren | 1/dawn, *death ward* |
| Duskheart (purple) | Jetten | 1/dusk, *modify memory* |
| Thornheart (green) | Invictus | 1/dawn, *raise dead* (dead ten days or less) |

Both ways need all four shards at the tree: hers to absorb them, Shar's to drain them. Every holder has a veto.

## PART FOUR · WHAT THEY CAN DO

### A · Free her

**What it takes.** All four hearts, placed in the knot one at a time. Each one takes an action at the knot and turns a quarter of the tree gold. On the fourth, she's free.

**The town objects.** They all heard her ask last night, and they object anyway. Oswin comes first. He tells them everything the elders know. There is no threat in him anywhere. He cries, badly, and apologizes for it. He offers what the town has, and knows it isn't enough. No roll changes his mind or theirs. If they say no, he thanks them, goes home, and calls out the watch.

| Moment | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Sitting down | "I heard her too. We all did. I've been up all night with the elders, and I'm going to ask you for something anyway." |
| The ask | "Don't give them back. Please. Everything we have comes from her. If she goes, we go." |
| He cries | "I'm sorry. I'm fond of you. All of you. That's what makes this so hard." |
| What he offers | "The town's coin. A house. A place at every table in Greenrest, for as long as you live. I know it isn't enough." |
| If they refuse | "Thank you for listening. I mean that." He goes home. |

**The fight.** Warin leads the militia: arcane casters, behind the Mob. The Mob is one swarm-style token with 350 HP and a Multiattack of five attacks, one of each: pitchfork, club, sword, thrown cabbage, thrown tomato. Like a 5e swarm, it can't be knocked prone, grappled or restrained. A melee hit can knock out instead of kill; arrows and spells can only kill. Tell the table that before initiative.

**At night.** If they try it quietly, Warin's double guard and Enid are already at the knot. The first heart lights the tree, the whole town sees it, and the square fills. Going at night buys a head start, not a way out of the fight.

**How it ends.** On the fourth heart, the knot opens and she steps out of the trunk holding the whole heart. Then THE LINE, word for word, slowly:

> *"It's stone. It has been stone in your hands for months — warm, and hard, and beating, and not once have you been able to leave a mark on it.*
>
> *She closes her hands around it, and it gives.*
>
> *The light goes out of the facets. The edges lose their shape. It goes soft, and warm, and wet — and by the time she lifts it to her chest it isn't a jewel at all. It's a heart. Plain, red, ordinary meat, beating in her hands.*
>
> *Because the stone was never the magic. The stone was the part of her that couldn't die."*

Then stop talking. Let someone at the table get there out loud.

> **NARRATE.** She puts the heart back where it came from. She looks at the square, at the town and at you, and she isn't angry with anyone. The tree blooms, every branch at once, once. Then the light goes out of it, and it's just a tree.

> **NARRATE (the grey dawn).** By morning the leaves are turning. At the edge of the valley, for the first time in a thousand years, there's fog.

**Afterwards.** If nobody in the Mob died, the town mourns its summer, not its people, and Oswin shakes their hands and can't speak. If anyone died, Coll finally has something to put in the grave he "just felt like digging." The Heart goes with her, and nobody gets any resurrections.

### B · Midnight into the knot

**What it takes.** Midnight and all four shards at the tree. Invictus drives the blade into the knot.

**The town supports it.** They all heard her ask last night, and they choose their summer anyway. Oswin cries with relief and can't look at the tree. Warin clears the square for him. Nobody fights, and nobody in the square has to die.

> **NARRATE.** The blade goes into the knot as if the wood had been waiting for it. The warmth goes out of the bark, out of the four shards, and up the steel into your hand. The shards go grey and cold. The blossoms close. The tree stays green, and stops. When you pull Midnight out, it's heavier than it was. And it beats.

**How it ends.** The Dreamer is trapped under the square forever. The four shards are dead stone, and their powers are gone. Midnight holds five resurrections, and Invictus takes it home to Daggerford to use them (see the epilogue). Greenrest keeps the Dreamer's summer forever: there's a feast, a key to the town, and Invictus is named a friend of Greenrest.

### C · Walk away with the shards

**What it takes.** Nothing. The shards can only be joined at the knot, so keeping them means keeping four separate shards, each with its own power, and no resurrections for anyone.

> **NARRATE.** You leave Greenrest with the four shards in your packs. The town keeps its summer. The tree keeps its buds and never quite blooms. And wherever you sleep, every night, she asks again.

She isn't sealed, only left. Someday someone else could carry the shards home.

### D · The party can't agree

No PvP, ever. Both ways need all four shards at the tree, so nobody can do either one alone, and nobody takes a shard by force. The split gets settled by talking, and each holder decides what happens to their own shard. If even one holder won't give theirs up, neither way can happen, and it ends as C. Let the argument be the scene. It's the one the whole campaign has been building.

### E · Jetten uses the Duskheart on Invictus

The Duskheart casts *modify memory* once per dusk. Shar's offer is less than a day old and lasted under ten minutes, so Jetten can erase it or rewrite it. Invictus gets a Wisdom save.

The elf who spoke Shar's words takes them back with Shar's own shard. If it works, Invictus forgets the offer. Shar called it her last offer and she meant it: Midnight goes quiet. The party remembers what they did to him.

### F · Raise Pudgy before they leave

The Thornheart raises one body per dawn, dead ten days or less, and Invictus holds it. The squirrels are in the great hall, and Pudgy is wearing Longshadow's feather. Honeysuckle and Pinenut would each need a dawn of their own. It has to happen before B: once Midnight drains the shards, the Thornheart can't raise anyone.

Pudgy, waking: *"Did we win? … Where's Honeysuckle?"*

The geased druid is down, not dead. With Bramblemaw dead, the compulsion is broken. Alive, he's a witness to what the dragon did in the Wyrmwood.

## PART FIVE · EPILOGUE

Two endings: she's trapped (B), or she's released (A). If they walk away (C), the ending is in C.

### IF THEY TRAP THE DREAMER

**Invictus**

> **NARRATE (Daggerford, the post-credits scene).** A month later, in Daggerford. Five graves in a row. He draws Midnight and says their names, one at a time. He can say all five. He still can't say his own. The ground gives them back.

> **NARRATE.** Ann calls him Thomas, the way she always did. The children call him Dad, and sometimes Thomas when they're teasing him. He answers to Dad. One morning the youngest asks if he remembers the day he made French toast and dropped the whole jug of syrup on the kitchen floor, and everyone laughs, and he laughs with them. He doesn't remember it. He never will. There are a lot of days like that. But they're at his table, all five of them, and he knows that much. Most days, that's enough.

**Jetten**

> **NARRATE.** Jetten doesn't come to the feast. By the time anyone thinks to look for him, he's on the Trade Way, going south. He finds new company, the way elves do eventually. He doesn't write. When someone mentions Thomas, he changes the subject. The moonstone never glows again.

His last words to Invictus, if there's a goodbye at all: *"You got five. She didn't get one."* ("She" is his sister.)

**Morgash**

> **NARRATE.** Word travels: the dragon, the temple, the choice at the tree. Months later, a shadow crosses the sun wherever Morgash has ended up. A red dragon, and on its back, Lae'zel.

*"I said I could not love anyone in this world. I have been at war since. I was wrong about this world. There is a place at my side, my Morgash. Ride with me."* A place beside her on the red dragon, in the wars to come. Whether he goes is up to Anthony.

**Gren**

> **NARRATE.** The squirrels stop coming. Not one in the whole Wyrmwood will come down a tree for him now. Word reaches the warren, and the elders send word back: don't come home. It's the second time they've cast him out, and this time the forest agrees with them. He sealed a fey creature into a tree for good, and nothing that lives under leaves forgets that. The Everseed Acorn went grey in his pocket the day the sword went in. He can't plant it anywhere now.

**Greenrest**

> **NARRATE.** The summer never turns. There is no autumn, ever again, and the children grow up without seeing a leaf fall. The town ties harvest ribbons around the scar in the knot. The dreams stop, and Greenrest sleeps well for the first time in a generation. Selma pours the tonic out. Nobody hears her anymore.

### IF THEY RELEASE THE DREAMER

**Invictus**

> **NARRATE.** As she goes, the Dreamer looks at him and says one word: *"Thomas."* Shar took his name, and her last gift gives it back.

> **NARRATE (Daggerford, a month later).** Five graves in a row. He reads every name on them out loud, and then his own, because he can say it again. He stays until dark. He grieves properly this time, and this time it ends.

**Jetten**

> **NARRATE.** That night the moonstone glows green one last time, the same glow as in the early dreams that nobody ever explained. He dreams of his sister, and it's a real dream, sent by Sehanine or by the Dreamer on her way out. He gets to say goodbye to her instead of getting her back. In the morning the stone is milky white again.

**Morgash**

> **NARRATE.** Bramblemaw's gems are the big score, the one he was a single job away from. He retires. The old Lower City gravedigger and Coll dig the Kestrels' memorial together, and do it properly. When the fog comes back to the edge of the valley, Warin asks him to hold the gate at Hedgewatch. He has held a gate before.

**Gren**

> **NARRATE.** Every squirrel in the wood knows Gren the Mighty freed the Green Lady. Word reaches the warren, and for the first time since the fire surfaced, the elders send for him: come home. He goes, for one feast. Then he comes back to Greenrest and plants the Everseed Acorn in the square, beside the old tree. Warren tradition says you plant it when you find the place you're meant to stay. It will grow into an ordinary oak that loses its leaves every autumn.

**Greenrest**

> **NARRATE.** The leaves fall in the square for the first time in living memory. The children have never seen it; they rake them into a pile and jump in, and Oswin watches from the steps of the Mayor's Tower. The fog sits at the edge of the valley now, and a few families take the Trade Way south before winter. Then, at the bedside of a sick child, Enid prays the way she always has, and for the first time in Greenrest's history, something answers. The harvests will be smaller from now on, but they'll be real, and the town finally has a healer of its own.
