## 1 · Homecoming — the town line at sundown

Open where they stood up: the edge of Greenrest, just before sundown, a scout waving them in.

- **The welcome is warm but the mood has shifted a degree.** At two shards the town is at
  *unease under the wonder* (§5.4): people have connected the blooming to the party's trips and
  are beginning to say so — pointed questions, still friendly. Nobody is hostile; everybody is
  *curious* now, and curiosity is new.
- **Business burst, kept lean** (arrive → business → turn in, so the dream lands fresh):
  - **The Tideheart.** First thing the table will raise. All four are at 3/3 attunement —
    someone drops something or it stays in the stash. Let them argue; do not solve it for them.
    (If someone unattunes to carry it: 2024 rules, attunement ends after a short rest spent
    focused on ending it, or when the item is no longer carried during a long rest.)
  - **Selling the fen valuables** (~3,100 gp of teacups, silk, paintings, dragon bones,
    tapestry) — Wend buys at her usual rates; the *volume* raises eyebrows and feeds the
    pointed-questions mood. "Where exactly did all this come from, dears?"
  - **The gilt-framed portrait IS Renlow Veck.** The hags kept a likeness of their supplier —
    the man who is, right now, chained under the Temple of Shar, painted fat and prosperous on
    the money he made selling them people. **Wend recognizes him.** He was a Full Larder
    customer: a Daggerford man who came through two or three times a year with "little boxes
    of sundries," sold some to her, took the rest out the fen road and came back lighter. She
    fetches her stock-book and reads the line: *R. Veck, Daggerford* — last visit about six
    months ago, headed home. So **Thomas gets the NAME in town**, night one, from a
    shopkeeper's ledger — and walks into the temple hunting a man he can name. *Thomas still
    never saw his face*; the cell payoff stays: they walk in and a player says "that's the man
    in the painting." Wend knows nothing past the name and the boxes. The stash item carries Veck's own
    art, so the recognition is something a player can *see*, not something the DM has to assert.
  - **Volo's Guide (Annotated)** — if anyone opens it at the inn, read the three-hands easter
    egg (spidery, blocky, small — "wrong. wrong. WRONG." / "…fair"). One melancholy beat, now
    that all three hands are dead.
- **Turn in.** Ambrose has the good rooms ready. No tonic tonight — nobody offers it to them
  anymore; the whole town knows the heroes don't take it.

## 2 · Night one — Dream A.2, "The Summer"

Fires the first night in real beds. SHARED — all four stand in it together, wherever they're
each sleeping. Read straight through, no checks, no interruptions. Full read-aloud in the table
script §2. Beats: the hillside in high summer · the raven-haired man walking up · seasons
turning, silver threading the hair, she never changes · the two under the tree, neither
smiling — *he will grow old and die* · her line: **"Stay. Stay, and I'll make it summer for you
forever. Be as I am. Never end."** · they wake, all four at once, morning.

- **No present-her in this dream** — every version of her is memory, absorbed in him, unaware
  of her audience. The party is theater-goers, not participants (A-appendix governing rule).
- **Jetten's moonstone** glows green the whole night. Again. Still unexplained.

## 3 · The morning after — buds, and a town that dreamed

- **The tree budded overnight.** Hundreds of fat green buds, first blossoms white as a held
  breath. The party hears the chatter before they see it. The town thinks it's a miracle.
  *They know it's a count.*
- **The town half-dreamed.** First time in years — nobody can remember what. Deliver it as
  breakfast-table gossip (Ambrose, rubbing his eyes: "whole house slept badly, and Wend says
  the same of her street"). Something is stirring. The party will connect it; let them.
- **Pointed questions, still friendly.** Pick ONE elder for the pressure beat — suggested:
  **Oswin**, warm as ever, over breakfast: he's heard what they sold Wend, he's seen the tree,
  and he asks — fatherly, no edge — *what is it you keep bringing home?* What they tell him is
  up to them; whatever it is, he thanks them and means it. (This is the tier-2 rung of the §5.4
  ladder — the tier-3 confrontation belongs to session 8.)
- **Destination.** The party knows the map: green (southwest, the dragon) and black (far
  north, the temple). If the table needs a nudge north: Thomas's ledger — the drop-stone and
  the supply chain ran *north through the moors*, and the hags' notes said the black shard was
  "not yet found" BY THE HAGS — but the ruin's location is on Sera's map. Rumor in town runs
  hot on the dark temple (travelers' stories of a sunless ruin) and cold on nothing. If they
  insist on the dragon: swap sessions 7/8 site content; the homecoming beats stay as played.

## 4 · The Temple — parley first, and the temptation in three movements

*Site: sunless, half-buried temple to Shar in the forest's far north. All living humanoids —
no undead (the Hollow owns undead). They know exactly what the shard is: grief made solid, a
sorrow that never runs dry — not a curse but a wellspring. The Duskheart is not for sale and
never will be; the prisoners are not a trade for the shard — they are a trade for Thomas.*

### 4.1 · Approach and structure

- **Nobody invites them in; the doors stand open.** No watchers
  on the stairs, no greeting party. They walk in on their own feet. Walking in armed is
  fine; the priest wants an audience, not a corpse.
- **First room: Harrow Vane meets them** and welcomes them himself — *no need for violence
  unless you want it.* He opens on **Thomas's mission and the Lady's admiration of him**;
  the Duskheart is an aside ("oh — and we have the Duskheart, too, if we get around to
  that"). The temple interior: candle-dark, purple-and-black, the congregation quiet and
  unafraid. Movement 1 happens in the sanctuary. The cells are below.
- **The Duskheart is NOT on the altar in the open.** **Harrow carries it on his person,
  secretly.** It surfaces
  when he chooses — or as loot off his body.
- **Combat is the failure state, not the content** — same rule as the dragon. When it comes it
  should come fast and hurt. Forces: the priest (caster, CR ~5–6), an
  enforcer pair, acolytes. Some surrender. One begs. One is very young. (§6.3 — whatever the
  party does here, they will be asked to do again in the square.)

### 4.2 · Movement 1 — the reading

The priest reads Thomas before a blade is drawn — vengeance-sworn, Lathander's colors, Oghma's
symbol — and opens with a compliment, and it is true:

- *You have been making a god's work out of your grief for years, and calling it dawn.*
- They are **impressed** — they recognize a colleague. And more than that: **they have watched
  him.** They saw the scribe take the oath. They saw what the rage built. A measly scribe to a
  paladin who breaks hags — Shar has been watching that climb with real admiration.
- The theology, offered sincerely: *a grief that is allowed to finish is spent; a grief kept
  broken never runs dry.* (They have just described the valley, and the party if it keeps the
  Heart. Do not point at it.)
- **The hook under all of it — oblivion as MERCY:** this is
  how Shar recruits nearly everyone she has. Grief *hurts*. She doesn't ask you to love the
  dark — she offers to **take the memory that's hurting you**, and calls it kindness, because
  from inside the pain it *is* kindness. The priest should say some version of it to Thomas
  straight: *you've carried this for years — she can carry it for you.* Play every cultist in
  the building as someone who took that deal and is genuinely grateful. (And it primes the
  turn without the players seeing it coming: Midnight taking his name isn't a price extracted
  — it is the mercy, delivered. She took the name his wife used to say. He feels *lighter*.)
- **The proof:** **zone of truth is not offered** — it is *available*: if the
  party says they don't believe him, Harrow casts it before the whole party and stands in it,
  and answers anything. **Let it work.** No save-cheat, no loophole. The horror of the scene
  is that they are telling the truth.

### 4.3 · Movement 2 — the murderer

They had **nothing to do with the murders** — say it plainly in the zone; it is true. But they
**have the murderer**. One man. Kidnapped by Shar's people **six months ago, within weeks of the day** he killed
Thomas's family — which is why the hags' shipments stopped. He has been their prisoner since.

- **The reveal is physical, not verbal.** They take Thomas (and whoever follows) down to the
  cells and show him the man. Recognition beat here — someone knows the face from the gilt
  frame, and Thomas already has the name from Wend's stock-book (§1).
- **The proof is complete.** The ledger cipher in his own hand. The dates. His confession,
  freely given in the zone of truth, sobbing. There is no ambiguity to hide in — this is him.
- **The man is broken.** Tied, begging for his life, saying he is sorry, saying it over and
  over. Half a year in Shar's cells has left nothing defiant to punch. This is exactly what they
  want: an execution, not a fight — vengeance at its purest and cheapest.
- **The pitch, said aloud:** *"Thomas — this is your oath. This is your power. Become
  vengeance."* They will put the blade in his hand if he lets them.
- **The stakes, cleanly:**
  - If he kills the man **in Shar's name / as they urge** → the conversion takes, and it takes
    **immediately, at the table**:
    - **First Light recedes.** The vision of a Lathander paladin is gone — the god himself
      slinking away from any connection with Thomas and his murder. No thunderclap, no wrath:
      *withdrawal*. The dawn just stops being in the room.
    - **The sword transforms into Midnight.** The rose-gold turns to inky darkness. **+2
      bonus, +1d8 necrotic damage per hit**. Heroism is gone; in its place **Death Armor
      1/dusk** (Heroes of Faerûn, 2nd-level necromancy,
      `Compendium.dnd-heroes-faerun.options.Item.hofDeathArmor000`) — touch a creature and
      an **inky aura** surrounds it for an hour: advantage on death saves, and melee
      attackers take necrotic once per turn. Heroism's exact shape — a protective touch
      buff — poured out of the dark instead of the dawn.
    - **Shar takes the name.** He loses all memory of his first name — cannot remember the
      word "Thomas" at all. He knows his whole life, everything around the name, every face
      in it — but the name itself is gone, taken by the Lady of Loss as her price. He is no
      longer Thomas. He knows himself only as **Justiciar Invictus**.
    - He does not become an NPC; he becomes a paladin whose oath has a new patron, and
      session 9 hits him differently. **Robert keeps the sheet; the table keeps the horror**
      — the party watches their friend fail to answer to his own name.
  - If he refuses, or kills the man on his own terms and Lathander's — he remains good, on
    Lathander's side. The priest is **not offended**. Shar is patient; the offer stands for
    the rest of the campaign (§6.3).
  - If he takes the man alive for lawful justice — bigger than any fight in the campaign. Let
    it be logistically hard (a prisoner across two days of moor) and let it be worth it.
- **DO NOT RESOLVE IT.** No Insight DC dissolves this. The other three players will have
  opinions — let the table argue. This scene is the session.

### 4.4 · Movement 3 — the fifth beat

While they have his ear — the strangest, sincerest offer of the campaign:

- They know what the Heart does whole: four beats, four resurrections. And they know Thomas's
  arithmetic: **five dead.** Ann, and four children. The Heart, as it stands, makes him choose
  which of his children stays dead.
- **Shar's offer: they will help him extend the Heart's power to resurrect his entire
  family.** All five. The rite is theirs, the price is nothing — *from him*.
- Why would Shar pay for this? Answer honestly if anyone asks (they're in the zone): the Heart
  belongs to a sleeping **archfey — a demigod — a close ally of Chauntea and Selûne.** Every
  beat spent is drawn from her; a heart stretched to five and emptied is a heart with nothing
  left to return. Using it **is** denying her release. *Shar would very much like her ally's
  friend kept tortured for eternity in grief and loss.* The resurrections are real. The
  generosity is real. The cruelty is the point, and they do not hide it.
- This lands the §7.4 dark-alternative arithmetic on the table **weeks early, priced in
  Thomas's own dead** — exactly the seeding the endgame doc asks for. Do not do the sum for
  the table; the Sharrans do it FOR Thomas alone, which is worse.

### 4.5 · The break — if and when it comes

- The Duskheart is not on the table and never was. Reach for it, insult the congregation, or
  try to free prisoners by force → the fight, fast and hard. The priest's first act: darkness
  and repositioning — this is their house and it has no windows.
- Standing constraints: all living; some surrender; one begs; one
  is very young. The party will be asked to do this again in the square (session 9) — run it
  so the rhyme is available, never stated.
- **The lift:** the Duskheart — dark purple, near-black, the light crawling under the surface.
  **Say the four-line tax: warm · beating · it will not take a mark** (§5.1 standing
  instruction — every shard, every time). First-hold vision: the heart-burst, verbatim.
- **The Duskheart as an item.** **Carry:** advantage on **Wisdom** saving throws
  (completes the trio — Graveheart CHA, Tideheart CON). **Once between dusks:** ***modify
  memory*** (5th), shard as the only component. One spell, and this is it.
  - **Why this and not a resurrection echo:** the siblings give life back (*revivify*) and hold
    death off (*death ward*). The Duskheart does the third thing, and it is Shar's actual
    doctrine rather than a power level — **it takes the memory that hurts.** It is the priest's
    §4.2 pitch made portable: *she can carry it for you.* Note the live wire this puts in the
    party's hands — someone can offer to take Thomas's grief, sincerely, to help. That is Shar
    winning through the party's own kindness, and it costs her nothing. Do not steer it; just
    let the item sit there being useful.

## 5 · The cells — the murderer ⚠ THOMAS · the heart of the night

- They lead Thomas down. The others may follow; nobody stops them.
- One man in a clean cell, chained to the wall. Six months a prisoner — taken within weeks of the murders. Nothing defiant left.
- **THE PORTRAIT IS HIM.** The gilt-framed merchant portrait out of the hag loot is Veck,
  painted fat and prosperous on murder money. **Let a player say it, not the DM** — the item
  art on the party sheet is his face, so somebody can simply *look*.
- **The proof is total:** the ledger cipher in his own hand · the dates · his confession,
  freely given, in the zone, sobbing. It is him. There is no ambiguity to hide in.
- He begs. By name if Thomas gives his. He is sorry. He says it over and over.

> **NARRATE — the cell (when they find him on the wall).** The man chained to the wall of
> the cell was somebody's clerk once — you can see it in how he holds his hands. Bare-chested,
> wrists bolted to the rock on either side, the coat and the cravat and the good waistcoat long
> gone. The only thing they left him is a gold signet ring on his right hand — worn smooth,
> and he keeps turning it against the iron. Half a year in this dark has taken everything else
> off him but the begging. He looks up at Thomas and he
> knows — you can watch him understand who this is — and he starts saying "I'm sorry" before
> anyone has said a word, and he does not stop.

- **The ring is the tell.** The portrait in the stash shows the same gold signet on the same
  right hand. Say "signet ring on his right hand" plainly and let somebody go open the item.
  If nobody bites, the priest can do it for them later: *"He wanted that painted, you know."*

> **NARRATE — the confession, in the zone.** _(He has had six months to arrange these words and
> nobody worth saying them to. Let it come out in pieces.)_
>
> "I sold names. That's how it started — only names. Widows, mostly. People with nobody left to
> come asking after them. It was money. It was only ever money."
>

> slow, and he was not going to stop." He will not look up. "So I went to his house to frighten
> him. That's the truth, and your spell will tell you it's the truth — I went there to put a

>

> doesn't do anything, does it. None of it comes back. I'd give it back. If there were a way to
> give it back, I would give it back."
>

**Playing Veck:** beaten, **not** redeemed. The sorrow is genuine — the zone proves it — and it
is still mostly about *him*: the six months, the dark, the thing he has to carry. The line that gives
him away is *"he should have left it alone,"* and he doesn't notice he said it. If someone calls
him on it he'll agree instantly, sincerely, and mean that too. There is nothing here to punch
and nothing to forgive — which is exactly what the Sharrans arranged. An execution, not a fight.

## 8 · Exploring the temple — numbered areas

_Read-aloud only: what they see, hear, smell, feel. No explanations in the boxes — let the
players guess what it's for. Numbers match the DM's marked-up maps. **01 the Iris** is
narrated in §4 and has nothing else in it._

### 02 · The Ceremonial Chambers

> **1** _(north-west room)._ A small, cold room. In the middle of the floor is a round hole
> rimmed in carved stone, the pattern an endless knot with no beginning. You cannot see a
> bottom. A dropped pebble makes no sound. Along the wall, crates of candles, unlit, by the
> hundred. The air coming up out of the hole is dry, and it smells of nothing at all.

> **2** _(west room)._ A brass sphere of rings as tall as a man turns slowly in the center of
> the room, and there is no hand on it and no wind to move it. The rings are set with silver —
> a moon, stars, a long empty arc between them. You look for the sun in it and there isn't
> one. Bookshelves on every wall. It is very quiet in here; the rings make no sound.

> **3** _(south-west room)._ A round table. Plain cups, a clay jug, a stack of loose pages
> weighted down with stones. The chairs are pushed in neatly. Jars along the wall, sealed with
> grey wax. It smells of ink and cold tea. Someone was writing here not long ago, and the ink
> on the top page is still a little wet.

> **4 — the sanctuary.** An eight-sided hall. Six long benches in purple velvet, angled in
> toward the middle — and in the middle there is no altar. There is a stair, curling down into
> the dark. Lamps burn low. The mosaic border at your feet is silver on black: a circle, and
> another, and another, each one empty. The benches face the stair. Whatever people come here
> for, they sit, and they look down.

> **5** _(east room)._ A carved figure bends over a stone basin: a woman, veiled, face turned
> down, her hands cupped under the surface. The water is black and does not move — not even
> when you lean over it. Something under the water is faintly violet. Two heavy chairs face
> the basin, close, like people sit here and talk to it. You catch yourself keeping your voice
> down.

> **6 — the cells** _(south, past the sigil room)._ A square room with a great gold seal in the
> floor — rings within rings — and a door on the far side. Beyond the door the cut stone
> simply stops, and it is cave: rough, sloping, cold. Three iron cages stand about the floor,
> and in each of them a skeleton is folded up small, dry and old and undisturbed. Purple
> mushrooms glow in a corner. The floor slopes down toward a carved stone rail across the far
> wall — and a man chained to the rock beside it. (§5.)

### 03 · The Grotto

> **7 — the lower hall.** The stair lets you out into a hall like the one above, but lower,
> quieter, older. Two round mosaics of gold on the floor, each under its own lamp — suns, by
> the shape of them, rays and all — and each one has been chiseled through at the center, a
> ragged hole where the middle should be. The chisel marks are old. Doors north, west, south.
> The air smells of cold stone and something earthy and sweet.

> **8** _(west)._ Bookshelves floor to ceiling. A desk with a lamp still burning and a chair
> pushed back as if someone just stood up. Two velvet couches. A reading table stacked with
> ledgers, ribbons marking places. Between the couches, a square stone plinth with a gold disc
> set into the top, the gold worn to dullness in one spot the size of a palm. There is nothing
> on it.

> **9** _(north)._ Crates and barrels, stacked square. A shelf of small stoppered jars, all the
> same. Candles by the hundred, lamp oil, salt, dried mushrooms strung on twine. It is tidy.
> It is stocked for a long time.

> **10** _(south cave)._ Fallen logs furred with mushrooms — purple, blue, a red so dark it's
> nearly black — and every cap of them faintly alight, so the whole cave glows without a
> flame in it. A long workbench: bowls, a pestle, a rack of jars, a knife. The smell is sweet
> and thick, and after a minute your eyes want to close.

> **11** _(north end of the long cave)._ The passage dead-ends at a round, stepped platform of
> pale stone, worn hollow at the center as if by many years of lying down. It is warm to the
> touch. You do not want to sit on it, and you can't say why.

> **12** _(south end of the long cave)._ The passage opens out, and the floor becomes a sigil:
> a great wheel cut deep into the rock and filled with blue and red, a disc at the hub and a
> glass orb at every spoke. When you step onto it, it hums — up through your boots, into your
> teeth. Crates in the corner. The walls close all the way round. There is no other way out.
> **⟨DM⟩ This is Harrow's *word of recall* point (§7) — if talk has broken, he is here, with
> everyone he brought.**
