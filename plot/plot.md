# The Broken Heart of Greenrest

*Campaign plot document — the single source of truth for story and canon. D&D 5e (2024 rules), four players.*

> A friendly village. Four ruins. A grief old enough to be mistaken for good weather.

> **Scope.** This file is the campaign's whole canon, as it stood when the story ended: what it says is what is true. Session records under `sessions/` are append-only table history and are never retconned to match it. Git history is the archaeology.

**The premise in one line:**

> *Under Greenrest sleeps a grieving fey. Its tears make the whole valley bloom — and the village that lives on them has no idea.*

That is the whole thing.

**The northstar — why the valley blooms.** Her last offer to Corin, made at the moment of his leaving and repeated into her sleep for a thousand years: *"Stay — stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever."* He didn't stay. **So she kept the promise to the only thing that never left: the land.** Greenrest's every soft winter and heavy harvest was summer-for-you-forever, given to a valley because it could not be given to him. Every scene that touched the bloom, the luck, the tree, or her was steered by this sentence.

**The loop.** Leave town → clear a ruin → claim its shard → limp home to Greenrest to rest, shop, and roleplay → watch the tree in the square wake a little further → dream a little more of the truth that night, in a real bed (§5.2) → pick the next site. The only number that mattered between sessions was **shards held**, and the tree showed it to everyone at a glance.

**How it ran.**

- **Level-up was the DM's call, never a shard payout.** Shards advanced the *story* tier (the dreams — §5.2); they bought no XP. Each ruin was tuned to the party's level when they arrived (they picked the order; the DM set the CR).
- Each ruin filled a session of its own, with town scenes between — Greenrest carried whole evenings by itself.

## 2 · The truth — the Dreamer and Corin

Long ago, before any village, a fey lived in this valley — an old, deep thing of spring and growing, the kind of power that makes orchards heavy and winters mild. Call it **the Dreamer**; it has forgotten its own name. It has forgotten almost everything. The only name left anywhere in its dreaming is **Corin** — a mortal it loved.

> **What the Dreamer looks like:** wherever she is glimpsed — in the dreams, and at the tree in the final passing — the Dreamer appears as a beautiful elven-fey **dryad**: tall and graceful, wood-and-blossom, with long blond hair. **She is blond fey; Corin is a raven-haired mortal — never conflate the two.**

Corin was a raven-haired mortal youth, brief the way mortals are. When Corin began to age — the first silver threading the black — the Dreamer, terrified, loving, not understanding, offered the only gift it had: *stay. Be deathless. Be fey, like me, and never end.* And Corin, who had lived among the fey long enough to see what they become — the slow hollowing, the centuries of drifting, the beautiful creatures that forget their own names — said **no**. *A life isn't for keeping; it's for spending.* He needed to go and live a mortal life, and he **left**: walked down out of her spring toward the fog. She followed exactly as far as she exists — to where her green meets the mists; a dryad ends at her valley — and the fog took him a step at a time. She never saw him again, and **she never learned what became of him**. Old, or happy, or nothing: his story ended somewhere past the fog, and no ending ever came back to her. *(What became of Corin is deliberately unwritten — nobody in the story ever learned it; see Appendix A.5.)*

So she waited — because she could not *understand*. To a thing of endless returning spring, someone who walks away and does not come back is gibberish: the green comes back, the swallows come back, everything comes back, always. Every year she made her spring more glorious than the last — not habit, *signal-fire*: a valley visible from far away, so it could be found again. Decades of springs. And then there came a spring when even she knew that a mortal's handful of years was long spent — that however his story had gone, it had *gone*, and she would never know which ending it wore. She broke on the not-knowing. Fey do not die; death is a door shut to them; she could not even follow him into that. So she did the only thing left to a creature that cannot die and cannot bear to live: **she shattered her own heart.** It broke into four true pieces, and the burst of it was the single most violent thing this gentle power ever did. The four pieces could not die either — a fey heart cannot, and rot is death's business — so, denied the one ending flesh is allowed, they **set**: grief gone hard, turning in the air to warm, translucent crystal. What came to earth in four far-apart places across the fog country was not gore. It was gemstone with a heart still inside it. The same detonation of grief **tore the mists apart above her valley** — blew the fey fog off it in every direction, the valley itself flinging back the fog that took him, one last time, too late — leaving a clearing in the Misty Forest that never closed until she was gone. Then, where she stood, she took root: she folded herself into her own dryad tree and went down into sleep, **hibernating forever inside her grief**, dreaming rather than living, because living had Corin's leaving in it. She slept a very long time, and she was still weeping when four hunters came down the Trade Way.

And here is the fey strangeness of it: her grief had nowhere to go but her magic, her refused promise had nowhere to go but the land, and her magic knows only one verb — *flourish*. Her tears didn't poison the valley; they **bloomed** it. A power of spring weeping is a land that cannot stop being fertile, lucky, green. Every soft winter and heavy harvest in Greenrest was a tear that fell a thousand years ago and was still landing — *summer-for-you-forever*, kept to the valley because it could not be kept to him (§1, the northstar).

**The geography of the grief was small.** The valley's magic — the bloom, the luck, the dreams — was the radius of the sleeping tree, and it ended where the mists resumed. The Misty Forest beyond was *not* in mourning; it is the same fey, foggy wild it always was. The only other touched places were — possibly — the four spots where the shards came to rest: a remnant of grief *can* cling to a shard's resting place or leak into whatever holds it, but that is a possibility, never a rule. One site wore it terribly (below the Hollow Shrine, the dead did not stay dead). At the others the shard was simply a thing of power that something found and claimed. Four bad places hidden in an ordinary wild, and one green valley with no mist in it — that was the entire footprint of what happened here.

> **The turn.** The village's luck was a person's unfinished grief. Gathered, the four shards made the heart whole — not to wake a monster, but to let a thousand-year mourner finally feel the whole of her loss, understand at last what Corin meant — *a life is for spending* — and be allowed to follow him into an ending of her own. The catharsis freed the Dreamer and killed the spell: Greenrest is withering, and the mists, held off a thousand years, have begun to close back over the valley. And a soul that was never permitted to die was, finally, free to.

## 3 · Greenrest, the innocent village

**There is nothing nefarious in Greenrest.** No conspiracy, no kept secret, no rule, no cage deliberately tended. It is exactly what it looks like: a lucky, kind, comfortable village that long ago stopped asking why — and everything the town did in this campaign, first to last, came from hospitality, habit, and (at the very end) fear for its own life. The village was never the villain. It was the cost.

### 3.1 · Founding, and the good life

Settlers working south down the Trade Way found something impossible in the fog country: a wide green valley with no mist in it at all, the best land anyone had ever seen, standing empty. They didn't know why the mists parted there, and they didn't ask — it was the only sustainable place to live for days in any direction, and the land was absurdly good. They stayed, and they flourished beyond all sense — soft winters, fat harvests, luck in everything — because they had built, without knowing it, inside the radius of a sleeping fey's magic. They called the place **Greenrest**: a green place to rest on a long road. Only the old families remember the quieter second reading — the green thing, resting, under the square.

The dreams came with the valley. Anyone who slept there dreamed grief — a weeping they couldn't place — and over the generations the town tied the dreams to the one wrong thing in its paradise: the pale, half-dead tree in the square. They guessed at what it was — something old sleeping under the tree, probably a dryad — and then, being practical people with a good life, they decided to live with it. They tended the tree, thanked the harvest, and let the mystery lie. Eventually the apothecary's craft produced a weekly draught that suppresses dreams; the whole town took it up as plain custom; and within a generation or two the dreams themselves were mostly forgotten. As far as Greenrest was concerned when the party arrived: there was a dead tree, there was a tonic, and life was good.

### 3.2 · What the town knew

Nothing here was guarded knowledge — there was only old understanding that most people stopped carrying:

- **The commoners** knew customs, not reasons. Everyone took Selma's tonic weekly from childhood (*"as soon as we're old enough to dream"* — Coll), the way no one explains the water. The gray tree was the town's odd, beloved symbol — *"the one thing that doesn't flourish; almost like a symbol of our town"* — tended, guarded, never quite explained. Bad dreams were a half-remembered thing the tonic solved long ago.
- **The elders** — Mayor Oswin Applewhite, Selma, Ambrose Featherstone, and Constable Warin Holt — still carried the older understanding, inherited, vague, and deliberately un-dwelt-on: the dreams are real and are the valley's; something old sleeps at the tree, probably a dryad; the tonic keeps its sorrow out of the town's sleep; and the valley's impossible luck is probably its gift. That was the whole of it.
- **Nobody** — elder or otherwise — knew about the shards, the Heart, or Corin; that the blooming legend the treasure-hunters chase had anything to do with their tree; or that the good life could ever end.

**The relic, as Greenrest knew it:** every so often an adventuring company passes through chasing a treasure-story about the old ruins in the forest — *"the Broken Heart,"* the **Kestrel Company** called it, the latest of them, half a year before the party came. What the town knew of the relic was exactly what such visitors had told them: a legend, a prize, four ruined sites. No one in Greenrest had seen it, sought it, or connected the name to anything in their valley. (Oswin, who spoke with the Kestrels, is the one who heard it from them.)

By the end, all of it was known: the whole town dreamed the breaking and the asking (§5.4), and watched her go.

### 3.3 · The tonic

Selma brewed the dream-suppressing draught — one swallow, seventh-day, as ever — and the entire village took it as unexamined custom. Nobody withheld it from guests; it simply didn't occur to anyone to press their grandmother's habit on a traveler. Selma sold it to anyone who asked. The town's dreamless sleep was the first true thread the party pulled — a village that drinks the same weekly draught and does not dream, in a valley that gives strangers nightmares, is a strangeness in plain sight, and an entirely innocent one. They slept without it on their first night in town (session 3). The tonic held until the dreams grew too strong: after the second shard came home the town began to dream again, and after the third it failed outright, double doses and all.

### 3.4 · The faith — Chauntea, the silent Earthmother

Greenrest openly worships **Chauntea, the Earthmother**, goddess of the harvest, thanking her for the bounty that never failed. But the goddess they praised was not the power that fed them. Every harvest-rite was truly aimed at the fey beneath the square, whom the villagers also revered under a gentler local name — **the Green Lady** — never grasping that the two are different beings. Two gods, one set of prayers; the town conflated them so many generations ago that no one alive could tell the green power under their feet from the green goddess in their liturgy. Even the elders, who suspected the thing at the tree was fey and no goddess, grew up inside the conflation; untangling a prayer that hurt nobody never seemed worth an afternoon. (The Green Lady is the Dreamer.)

**The theology — silence, not wrath.** Chauntea knew. She knew her name was fixed to a false idol, and that the valley's whole sweet life was drawn from a soul denied its death — the single unnatural thing the Earthmother, above all gods, cannot abide. So she refused: in all Greenrest's history before the passing she **never called a cleric, never granted a spell, never sent one miracle here**. Her refusal was aimed at the place, not the people — however innocently Greenrest prayed, the thing under it was the one thing she could not bless. And because Chauntea is the gentlest of gods, her refusal looked like *nothing at all*: no plague, no omen, an absence the town never even recognized as a refusal. Her silence was indistinguishable from the good weather they credited her for.

**At the table this meant:**

- **There was no divine healing in Greenrest.** No town cleric, ever; no child of the valley had knelt and received the Weave. Wandering clergy (Brother Tobin) passed through; when a local healer waved holly and prayed over Invictus's Hollowing (session 5), nothing answered.
- **The only cure in town was Selma's** — pure herbal alchemy, no god's grace in it, unusually potent because the valley's soil grows healing herbs as impossibly as it grows everything else. Every flask was the *Dreamer's* gift wearing the Earthmother's name — exactly like the harvests, exactly like the tonic.

**The silence ended.** After the passing, Enid prayed at a sick child's bedside, and for the first time in Greenrest's history something answered: Chauntea has forgiven the town. The harvests are smaller now, but real, and Greenrest has a healer of its own.

**The church is a clue in stone.** The **Church of the Earthmother** is an ornate cruciform basilica — carved tympanum, nave, altar, walled graveyard, priest's-study and infirmary wings. A humble harvest-goddess does not get a cathedral out of a farming hamlet; the grandeur is gratitude architecture, raised over generations out of the overflow of a sleeping fey's grief. It was full of worship and empty of divinity. What it handed the party:

- **The tympanum relief** — a winged, blossoming figure the locals call Chauntea, which Religion training clocks as *not* orthodox Earthmother iconography (no sheaf, no sun, no rose). It is the Green Lady wearing the Earthmother's name. The first crack. (Invictus's Investigation in session 5 found even the original stonework all fruits and farming — built *for* Chauntea by people who didn't understand her ways.)
- **Grove and fey trappings throughout** — too many, wrong for the Earthmother.
- **The walled graveyard** — the graves of the founders; and one fresh plot dug and never filled — Coll's, waiting for the **Kestrel Company**, whose bones never came home. A single grief, kept open.
- **The study and infirmary wings** — the infirmary is where the town's healer sent Invictus in session 5, when her prayers did nothing for him.

### 3.5 · The places of Greenrest

The village sits on a lake, ringed by orchards and hedged fields, visibly more prosperous than a village this size has any right to be. Locations (hub scenes from the Ostenwold set — §9.1):

- **Town Square** — the heart of the village; the Dreamer's dryad tree stands dead-center, four watchmen posted on it (orders from mayor and constable: nobody harms it, nobody chips the bark — reverence and prudence, nothing darker). The campaign tracker (§5.3), and the site of the finale (§7.2).
- **The Long Rest** — the inn (ground / upper / basement), kept by **Ambrose Featherstone**. Cozy on the sign, and — though nobody alive gives it a thought — the truest name in town.
- **The Full Larder** — the general store, **Mother Wend's**. Never runs empty in a valley that never failed a harvest. Stocks a small shelf of true magic items (the 400 gp tier); buyback at half.
- **The Apothecary** — **Selma's** shop. The town's only healing (potions) and the weekly tonic, sold over one counter.
- **The Mayor's Tower** — **Oswin's** seat and home, at the top of the hill ("the mayor always lives up at the top — it's tradition").
- **The Church of the Earthmother** — the basilica and graveyard (§3.4).
- **Hedgewatch Garrison** — the watch's stout blockhouse and palisade at the village's eastern edge, ~50 strong under **Constable Warin Holt**. Standing doctrine: *"we don't leave the town, and they don't come in here."* When the fog came back, Warin asked Morgash to hold its gate (§4).
- **The Blacksmith** — **Gully's** forge (background NPC; in session 5 he made Brakk's claw-rent plate wearable again, in trade for two of the Hollow's Necrotic Swords).
- Rounding out the town: a brew house, and the dockside on the lake.

### 3.6 · The cast

The ending only ached because the village had faces worth losing. All are built as neutral commoner actors in the world (`04 Greenrest › Main Cast`); Tobin lives with the Trade Way NPCs.

- **Mayor Oswin Applewhite** — the patron. Warm, fatherly, genuinely fond of the party from the first day; mayor some fifteen years, never left the valley, never faced an election ("the town just sort of votes whoever wants to do the bureaucracy"). Boasted, accurately, that *"there is no crying in Greenrest."* One of the four elders (§3.2). For almost the whole campaign there was nothing behind the smile but more warmth. He skipped his tonic to dream alongside the party, worked the truth out for himself, and at the end met them in the road and begged them to stop; when Pip turned him, he stood the watch down (§7.1). After the passing he asked them to go, and watched the leaves fall from the steps of the Mayor's Tower. Cornered, never malicious; he wasn't wrong, and that is what made it hurt.
- **Selma** — the apothecary. Brewed the tonic and the potions that are the town's only healing; an elder, and one of the few who knew what the draught actually kept out. Practical, unhurried, honest as far as her knowledge goes — the elder most likely to answer a straight question with the old half-truths, if asked kindly. She does **not** know the visiting hunters' "Broken Heart" legend; the mayor is the one who spoke with the Kestrels, and Pip's session-3 tip that she "doesn't shut up" about it was a small eavesdropper mixing up which adult retold the Kestrels' relic-talk. When the town began to dream she tried to brew the tonic stronger. Shown Renlow Veck's finger, she gave Morgash a Potion of Superior Healing on the house — and it saved Invictus's life in Bramblemaw's hall.
- **Ambrose Featherstone** — keeper of **The Long Rest**. A grandfatherly host who gave the party the good rooms and fretted over their wounds; an elder, and the last man in town to want to talk about old strangeness while there's supper to serve.
- **Mother Wend** — an elderly dwarf who keeps **The Full Larder**. Generous and decent, exactly what she seems; knew all four of the party's names before they ever walked in, because everyone in a town this size does. Her stock-book named **Renlow Veck** from the hags' portrait — a regular every couple of months, out on the Fen Trail with sealed boxes, back without them (§6.3).
- **Constable Warin Holt** *(heard at the table as "Warrenholt")* — watch-captain of Hedgewatch Garrison, and an elder. Friendly, protective, professionally embarrassed that his fifty men could do nothing about the thing that was killing travelers ("we don't leave the town"). He briefed the party on the displacer pack (§3.7), and once let slip that he thought he knew the Kestrels' mistakes — a theory he never got to tell. In the finale his watch never took the square; when the fog came back he asked Morgash to hold the gate at Hedgewatch.
- **Coll** — the gravedigger. Lifelong local; the man who should have buried the Kestrel Company and never got the chance — nothing ever came home to bury — and he has not been right since (*"it hasn't been right since"* they came through). The one commoner who never made his peace with the valley's unexamined things — the closest Greenrest has to a man who wonders out loud. First glimpsed leaning on his spade over a fresh, empty plot he "just felt like digging"; he skipped his tonic now and then and got the dreams for it.
- **Pip** — a young halfling child, too young for customs, old enough to like the party. Asked the questions no adult would, saw the things adults explained away (he watched the displacer pack circle the town for days: *"it had too many legs, so I didn't tell anybody"*), and refused gold on the grounds that he already had everything he needed here. The clearest face of what dies if Greenrest withers — and the reason "withers" was never a clean win. After the third dream he drew the four colours in berry juice in the dirt; in the road at the end it was Pip who turned the mayor: *"I think he's right, Mayor. They need to do what they need to do."*
- **Acolyte Enid** — the devout young lay-keeper of the church: leads the harvest-blessings, tends the nave and the graves kindly. She had never once received a sign from her goddess — no miracle, no spell — and it had never occurred to her that the silence might mean something. The purest believer in the wrongest faith. After the passing, at a sick child's bedside, her prayer was the first in Greenrest's history to be answered (§3.4).
- **Brother Tobin** — a barefoot, wandering halfling priest of Chauntea (not of Greenrest; met on the Trade Way in session 1). Orthodox enough to have already sensed the wrongness — *"some fields simply cannot fail, others starve a mile off; she doesn't usually play favorites"* — and doctrinally equipped to name it: wrong iconography, a too-grand church, and, beneath it all, a soul kept from death.

### 3.7 · The displacer pack — destroyed, and what it bought

For years a **Feywild-native displacer beast pack** hunted the forest approaches to the valley. (It slipped through the thin place at the ruined fey temple deep in the woods — origin lore, never revealed at the table; the temple's lintel-line was never read.) **The town knew about the pack all along** — this was never a secret anyone kept: Warin's scouts had tracked it for years, even Pip had watched it from the hill and counted too many legs, and everyone understood it was the reason visitors so rarely came back. The pack stalked the **Kestrel Company**, waited for a weak moment, and slaughtered them at their retreat camp — a ruined temple in the woods southeast of town, on the road back from the caves they had just fled — and it marked this party the same way, shadowing them from before they ever reached the village. Knowing was all Greenrest could ever do about it: the watch does not leave the town, and the pack never came in.

**The party ended it** (session 3): briefed by Warin, they chose their moment, hunted the pack to the Kestrels' own ruined camp, and destroyed it — three beasts and the alpha. One beast fled when the alpha died and is **gone for good** — no return, no echo.

**What it bought:** Greenrest knew exactly what that pack had cost it, and the party killed it. The welcome, already warm, became open gratitude — drinks stood, prices shaved, children trailing them through the square. The party were the heroes of the valley: the high-water mark the rest of the campaign fell from.

## 4 · The party

Four hunters out of Daggerford, following a map assembled from Gren's research toward a relic none of them understood. Their own griefs were the campaign's fuel — the night-zero dream mirrored each one back at its owner, and the Heart's final arithmetic (§7.4) was built on two of them.

- **Thomas A. Invictus** — human paladin of Lathander (Oath of Vengeance), 28, Daggerford native, former scribe; carried a Holy Symbol of Oghma besides, and the **Lantern of Revealing**. His **wife Ann and four children — five people — were murdered** six months before the campaign by **one man, Renlow Veck**, a Daggerford shipping clerk he'd discovered selling names and body parts to the Widow Fen hags; investigating him is what surfaced the Broken Heart. Veck went to the house meaning to *frighten* a careful man off the trail, and it went further; the Sharrans took him within weeks of the murders and held him prisoner until the party came (§6.3). Invictus bore **First Light** (a Dawnbringer's +1 longsword, Heroism 1/day) until the Temple of Shar, where he killed Veck in his cell: First Light became **Midnight**, and **Shar took his first name** (§6.3, §9.2). His grief was the Temple's lever, and he had more dead than the Heart had beats (§7.4). He carried the Thornheart out of the Wyrmwood, refused Shar's last offer, and on the lakeshore Lathander came to him in the fog and told him to bury the sword; Midnight is in the ground there (§7.4). After the passing he said his name again — *"Thomas. My name is Thomas."* A month later in Daggerford he made peace with Lathander's temple, read the five names aloud at the graves and then his own, stayed until dark, and the grief ended. A paladin again, he went wandering with Jetten for a few years.
- **Jetten Elisedil** — wood elf ranger, 110 years old, Invictus's childhood friend. His **sister died long ago**, and outliving her never healed; she was the other name in the Heart's arithmetic (§7.4). Bears **Goldthorn** (a fey Gilded Scimitar, Faerie Fire 1/day) — and a **moonstone necklace that glowed bright green whenever the Dreamer's dreams took him**. He carried the Duskheart from the Temple of Shar, and it spoke through him once (§7.4). After the passing the moonstone glowed green one last time: a real dream of his sister, sent by the Dreamer. He said goodbye, and in the morning the stone was milky white. He kept wandering, and stayed Thomas's friend.
- **Morgash the Gravemaker** — orc fighter (Battle Master), ex-Flaming Fist out of Baldur's Gate, one big score from retirement. **Lae'zel left him** — *"I cannot love someone in this world… goodbye, my Morgash"* — the heartbreak he kept private: after the shared dream he admitted the weeping woman and refused the details. (The Widow Fen hags had been drawing her face for years: Lae'zel killed their sister — §6.4.) He carried the Graveheart. Bramblemaw's gems were the big score, and he retired on them; when the fog came back he took Warin's offer to hold the gate at Hedgewatch, because he felt responsible. Greenrest forgave him in time: Oswin stands him breakfast and dinner, and he keeps a permanent room at the Full Larder.
- **Gren Greenmantle** — forest gnome sorcerer (Spellfire), raised in the Misty Forest and **cast out** when the fire surfaced — "unwanted, unloved" is his dream's shape. He found the shard references in an old temple ledger; the party's map is his work. Suspicious by temperament (the tonic-free night was his idea), and sworn to a standing vendetta against will-o'-wisps. He carried the Tideheart. To the squirrels of the Misty Forest he is **Gren the Mighty**, and **Pudgy** — raised by the Thornheart after Bramblemaw's breath killed him — is his companion for good. Every squirrel in the forest knows Gren the Mighty freed the Green Lady; the warren that cast him out sent for him, and there was a feast. Then he planted his **Everseed Acorn** in the square beside the old tree and stayed, with Pudgy as prophet of a small squirrel cult. Gren, Morgash and Pudgy are the Three Amigos; Gren's old debt to Morgash is paid in acorns by the bedside every morning, and Morgash steps on them half the time.

## 5 · The engine — shards, dreams, tree, town

Four systems, one mechanism: every shard carried home pulled the Dreamer's heart a step closer to whole. The **dreams** sharpened (truth arrived), the **tree** woke (everyone saw it), and the **town** reacted (to what it saw and what it was told). Shard count drove all three; nothing depended on *which* ruin was cleared, so the four sites truly ran in any order.

### 5.1 · The shards

**What a shard is — crystal, not meat.** Each is a piece of clear, faceted stone the size of two cupped hands: a quarter of a heart caught mid-burst and turned to gem. It was described the same way at every site:

- **It is crystal, and it is obviously still a heart.** The chambering is there in the stone — the wall of it, great vessels sheared off clean, something like muscle fibre suspended inside like a flaw in an emerald. Nobody who holds one doubts what they are carrying a quarter of.
- **It is warm.** Stone should be cold. This is blood-warm, always, warmest against a bare chest.
- **It beats.** Once every few seconds, felt in the palm before it is seen — and when it beats, the light inside *moves*, a slow pulse dying at the facets' edges.
- **It never chips, scratches, or breaks.** It has already been broken; that happened, and nothing since has been allowed to matter.

*Why crystal:* she could not die, so neither could her heart. Denied rot, it set — the gemstone **is** the deathlessness, made handleable.

> **The payload.** The warm-beating-unmarkable description was the campaign's **only planted payload**, said at the shards and spent at the finale in THE LINE (§7.3), where the crystal softened back to flesh in her hands. Four boring repetitions bought one unforgettable moment.

First held, it shows a vision: the heart-burst, the mists parting over an empty valley, the gray tree. |
| **Thornheart** | Venom green — the exact green of **Bramblemaw**, the dragon curled around it, which is either a coincidence or isn't | The Wyrmwood | Advantage on DEX saves; 1/dawn, pressed to a body dead no more than ten days, it casts *raise dead*. Its one use raised Pudgy (§4). First-hold vision: the heart-burst, the ring of mist driven back, the grey leafless tree. |
| **Duskheart** | Dark purple, near-black, the light crawling *under* the surface | The Temple of Shar | Advantage on Wisdom saving throws; **1/dusk *modify memory*** (shard is the sole component). Deliberately *not* a resurrection echo — where the Graveheart gives life back and the Tideheart holds death off, the Duskheart does Shar's own work and **takes the memory that hurts**. |
| **Tideheart** | Deep water blue, like light rising through black water | The Widow Fen | Mirrors the Graveheart: advantage on CON saves; 1/dawn casts *death ward* (cold salt water flooding the lungs and receding); first-hold vision identical to the Graveheart's heart-burst vision, verbatim. |

All four require attunement. In the end they were carried by Morgash (Graveheart), Gren (Tideheart), Jetten (Duskheart) and Invictus (Thornheart).

**The shards remember.** They are pieces of a heart, and a heart holds a person. The more shards the party carried, the more of Corin's story leaked back into their sleep — reassembling the heart was reassembling the memory. This was the reveal's engine, and it was gated by **count, never by site**.

**The whole Heart** is the relic the legend promised: joined as one it grants **four castings of *true resurrection* — one per piece** — among other powers never spelled out. But its magic *is* the Dreamer's unfinished grief, which made the endgame a true choice: bring it to the tree and she takes it back into herself — **the relic dies with her** (§7.3); keep it, and it stays at full power exactly as long as she is left weeping (§7.4). The miracle, or her release. Never both. The party chose her release.

### 5.2 · The dreams — where the truth comes out

The Dreamer's sorrow soaked her valley, edge to mist-wall edge. **Anyone who slept in Greenrest dreamed of grief** — except the villagers, who had taken Selma's tonic weekly for generations and no longer dreamed at all. So the newcomers dreamed and the town didn't, and nobody in town thought that worth mentioning, because nobody in town remembered it. That held until the second shard came home; the town began to dream that night, and from the third dream on the whole town dreamed with the party.

**Ambient nights (0 shards, and any night without a new shard).** The dream that visited every unshielded sleeper: the same **pale woman, shaped like a white tree, hair of leaves and branches, weeping** — and her grief bleeding into the dreamer's *own*, mirroring each sleeper's private loss back at them. Personal, but recognizably the same visitor: a party comparing notes at breakfast finds they all hosted the same weeping figure. (This is exactly how night zero played — see §8; the figure is identifiable, on a strong Religion check, as a **dryad**.)

**Reveal nights (a new shard carried home).** The dream stopped visiting and started *hosting*: **one shared dream, one place, all four sleepers standing in it together** — able to see one another, speak, move, react, played as a group scene, never four narrations. It quietly told the truth, too: a private dream is your own mind, but a dream you can all walk into is a *place* — someone else's, reaching out. From the third dream on, the town stood in it with them.

The reveal dream delivered the next tier of Corin's story, by **current shard count**:

| Returning with… | The dream gave them |
|---|---|
| **1 shard** | **The Glimpse.** They stand unseen inside her memory: a summer hillside, a raven-haired face laughing, a feeling of being adored that isn't theirs. **She is unaware of them** — the warmth is ambient, the ache borrowed. *(As played, session 5: she called them "Corin" — stay this time — and on the second night, when Jetten held out the Graveheart, her eyes widened and the dream cut. See A.1 for the canon reading.)* |
| **2 shards** | **The Summer.** The love in full: the meeting, seasons of it, the valley blooming harder every time he smiles because she wills it for him; more silver in his hair each pass, none in hers; and at the end the two of them under the tree, neither smiling, both knowing he will grow old and die. Her wish said aloud — to him, never to them: *stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever — be as I am, never end.* (A.2; session 7.) |
| **3 shards** | **The Breaking.** The argument played to its end — the offer pressed in desperation, the refusal made final — and Corin walking into the fog. Then the waiting, in a strobe: decades at the fog-line, the valley's **last true seasons** wheeling past, springs made glorious as signal-fires, and no ending ever coming back to her. Then the spring she breaks on the not-knowing — and the **heart-burst, in full**: the Graveheart's first-hold vision with its whole story around it. The dream ends in the explosion. The whole town dreamed it too. (A.3; session 8.) |
| **4 shards** | **The Asking.** For the first time she **sees them** — and the whole town is there with them, no mist between. She forgives Corin (*he was right*) and herself, and begs them to free her: bring the hearts to her tree, find the heart knot, place them one by one; on the fourth, she will be free. That same morning, Oswin asked for the opposite (§7.1). (A.4; session 9.) |

The four dreams — scripts and what was delivered — are in **Appendix A**.

> **A reveal dream fired on homecoming, and followed the party.** The trigger was **sleeping in Greenrest, in a real bed** — not the shard, and not the session boundary. **Nights on the road were dreamless**, and that contrast was itself a clue delivered for free: the dreams belong to the *place*. So no session cold-opened with one on a schedule; each came where the party actually was, mid-session, while the table was fresh, with the day that reached the beds kept lean (arrive → business → turn in) so the dream landed with hours left to argue about it over breakfast. The dream was this campaign's entire truth-delivery mechanism (there was no exposition NPC). The fourth broke the pattern only in its hour: it took the party, and the whole town with them, as they crossed Greenrest's limits in the late morning.

**On in-between nights the dream could plead instead.** *The waiting*: she mistook a PC for Corin — *you came back; I knew you'd come back.* Tender, and terrible: she had been wrong for a thousand years and was about to be wrong again. (Session 5: *"Corin? …Stay this time."*)

The more shards they held, the more lucid and present every dream became — the Dreamer stirring as her heart reformed, hoping, in a way that got harder to be in the room for.

### 5.3 · The tree — the campaign's tracker, made flesh

The gray tree in the square **was the Dreamer** — not a marker over her; *her*. A dryad and her tree are one life, and this tree was the shape her hibernation wore. It should have been the most magnificent thing in the valley; instead it was the one thing there that was dying — pale, half-bare, clinging — because a dryad's tree cannot thrive with her heart in pieces and scattered for miles. The town tended it, guarded it, and called it a symbol without knowing of what.

Every shard carried into Greenrest was a piece of her heart brought back to its seat, and because the tree *was* her, it *felt* it: the first new buds in living memory at one shard (the town saw them before the party did); hundreds of buds overnight and a first blossom open at two; and, when the fourth heart went home, full bloom, every branch at once. Close to the tree, a carried shard beat harder; walking away, softer — the party tested it twice. Heart gathering, tree waking, dreams sharpening: three faces of one thing.

Now it is just a tree, dropping its leaves — the first leaves to fall in the square in living memory. Beside it grows the young oak from Gren's Everseed Acorn, which drops its leaves every autumn, as an oak should.

### 5.4 · How the town took it

Greenrest started the campaign knowing **nothing** about shards. What the town understood at any moment was the sum of **what the party had told people**, **what the tree was visibly doing**, and — from the second homecoming on — **what the town itself was dreaming**. It stayed hospitable, unsuspicious, incurious; nobody interrogated the heroes of the valley, and the party never named the hearts to Oswin. The unease, when it came, was the status quo moving: the one constant of every villager's life changing, week by week, since the strangers started hauling treasures home.

| Shards | The tree | Greenrest |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Sick, half-bare | Warm — and after the pack's death (session 3), openly grateful. Heroes' welcome. |
| **1** | First new buds in living memory | Wonder, and "double heroes" (session 5). Pip reported Selma nervous that the tree was growing. |
| **2** | Hundreds of buds overnight; one blossom open | A miracle in the square (session 7). Over eggs, Oswin asked what they kept bringing home; Morgash gave him the honest version, and Oswin agreed to skip his tonic. The town began to dream; Selma tried to brew the tonic stronger. |
| **3** | Blossoming | The whole town dreamed the breaking (session 8). The tonic failed; the children invented a game called *The Breaking*; Oswin worked out the valley's debt for himself and called the elders. |
| **4** | Full bloom, every branch at once | The whole town shared the fourth dream (session 9). Oswin begged in the road; Pip turned him; the town came anyway (§7.1–7.2). |

## 6 · The four ruins

All four sit in the **Misty Forest**, the fey-and-fog country around the valley — the four far places where her heart came to rest. They ring Greenrest exactly as the party's treasure map draws them: the **Temple of Shar** in the forest's far north; the **Widow Fen** at its near-northern edge, where the woods drown away toward the moors; the **Wyrmwood** southwest, in the deepest forest; and the **Hollow Shrine** southeast, in the hills that climb toward the High Moor. *(The treasure map handout is the geographic authority — conform all text to it.)* In every one the shard was a prize that claimed whoever found it: a dragon, a cult, a coven, and — in the Hollow Shrine — three treasure-hunters who got there first and were still there. **None of the sites has any connection to the village.** Each shard was guarded and each site deadly; that is why the shards sat unclaimed for centuries, and why the rare company that went looking tended not to come home. The party beat what held each one, took the shard, and carried home both a fragment they couldn't read and the dream that would read it for them.

> **Action economy.** A small party against a lone "kill on sight" guardian is swingy rocket-tag. Every guardian below had minions, adds, or lair actions.

> **Order as played:** the Hollow Shrine (session 4), the Widow Fen (session 6, reached at one shard), the Temple of Shar (session 7), the Wyrmwood (session 8).

### 6.1 · The Hollow Shrine — underground caves

*Five scenes in the world (`05 - The Hollow`): 01 Stairs Down → 02 Catacombs → 03 The Keeper's Seat (hub) → 04 Preparation Chamber → 05 Hollow's Heart; room-level detail lives in the world's `05 The Hollow — GM Key`. The party's first site, played in session 4 (18 Aug): the march out ran southeast, past the Kestrels' looted camp, into the hills toward the High Moor.*

- **The story inside:** centuries before Greenrest, **three friends** — treasure-hunters like every pair of boots since — found a red crystal heart glowing in a cave, and could not leave it. **Osric**, who spoke for them, had the shrine cut and a seat carved to keep watch from; **Hesper**, who tended their dead and laid the catacomb niches; **Cadoc**, who stood closest watch over the altar — so he died first, and rose first, and the other two saw it and *stayed anyway*. The wanting took all three. The shrine became their tomb, and the Graveheart sat on its altar until the party came.
- **The guardians: five wights, no more** — the three founders, plus **Aldous and Edda**, the two Kestrel Company members who made it down here half a year before the party. **The Graveheart makes wights**: greed-born, the Gollum pattern — victims are readied on the chained slab in the Preparation Chamber and turned at the altar. The motive in the dark is that **they want more friends**: the heart makes loneliness, even for the dead, and a party of four is four empty niches' worth. The ambient shard-power raises only lesser dead (zombies, skeletons — the adds are built in). They sob, they never roar. Osric can talk — courteous and dead-tired until refused, imperious after, no monologues — and everything he offers comes around to *stay* (the insolent are promised zombiehood instead: friendship is an honor, the rest are stock).
- **The clock — the party escapes by not running out of *maximum* hit points.** Life Drain shaves the hit-point **maximum** with every hit; current HP heals back, but the ceiling returns only on a long rest, and there is no safe rest in the tunnels. Grief, mechanized: every drained hit makes there be permanently less of you until you leave. The tell: *"the wound closes; something underneath it doesn't."* Retreat-and-return was a legitimate play. The shrine is a race against the party's own shrinking ceiling, not attrition they can heal through.
- **The build's sharp edges** (full detail in the GM Key): Life Drain bites for **half even on a successful save**; the way down to the heart room is locked *against the unprepared* — Hesper's Bone Key and the **Draught of Wholeness** (the one cure for drained maximums) both wait in the Mortician's Chest, and Hesper *tells* visitors to take them, so the forced order is 03 → 04 → back → 05. Her room is a conversation, not a fight. And **Hesper could have killed the party's favorite theory**: they suspected Greenrest was behind the dead, and she is five centuries older than the village — asked the name, she would have answered, honestly, *"I don't know that word."* Nobody asked.
- **The trace it leaves:** three people found something warm in the dark, could not put it down, and it hollowed them out while they thanked it for the privilege. **They never learned what it was** — there is no image of the Dreamer anywhere in the shrine, no name, no story; anything they carved, they invented, and it is worn past recognition. The party's only route to her from this site was the Graveheart's own first-hold vision. And the deeper you go, the more the things in the dark *sob*.
- **As played:** all five rooms cleared and five wights destroyed; Osric refused parley and lost his head for it; Hesper the Mortician answered plainly and asked to be let go; Jetten lifted the Graveheart, and its vision came with it. Three of the four came out Hollowed, and the Hollowing is what finally dragged them into Greenrest's beds (session 5). The Hollow is spent: it does not restock.
- **Reward:** the Graveheart — carried, in the end, by Morgash.

### 6.2 · The Wyrmwood — Bramblemaw's lair

*One scene from the Tom Cartos **green-dragon-lair** set, 44×34. Bramblemaw's stat block was the DM's own. Played in session 8 (22 Sep).*

- **The site:** southwest of the valley, in the deepest forest — a stretch of woods gone wrong, sickly, briar-choked, too quiet, coiling in toward the lair of **Bramblemaw**, a green dragon that made the shard the centerpiece of its hoard. (The Thornheart is named for this wood: the Wyrmwood grew thorns around it the way a body grows scar tissue.) The lair itself is not a cave but a **drowned temple** older than anything in the valley, which the wood has swallowed, sunk half into a forest lake: a processional causeway standing in green water, a throne dais at its east end, a hoard room broken open to the caves above the waterline, dry ruins westward, and a squatters' camp in the undercroft where something had been living.
- **It would never give up the shard. Not for anything.** There was no price that worked and no deal to be struck — no Persuasion roll, clever trade, or generous player could buy it. The stone came off that hoard over Bramblemaw's body or not at all, and it came off over his body.
- **The trace it leaves:** a thing with no softness anywhere in it, bent to weeping over a prize that was never its own grief — unable to let go even when letting go would make it richer — and furious that it couldn't stop. The party never saw it weep: it met them with one line in Draconic — *"Come for my treasure, eh? You'll die instead."* — and its breath.
- **As played:** Sera's map and Oswin's memory of an old lake led them southwest, and three squirrels — **Pudgy**, **Pinenut** and **Honeysuckle** — went in to guard Gren. A Shambling Mound, a geased, horned druid (*"Must serve the master"*) and a giant crocodile; then four rounds across the flooded hall. Bramblemaw's first breath killed all three squirrels; Jetten and Invictus each went down twice; Selma's Superior Healing brought Invictus back; and Gren's Careful Fireball — *"This is for Honeysuckle, Pinenut and Pudgy"* — killed the dragon. The hoard, rolled straight from the tables: a Staff of Withering, a Decanter of Endless Water, a Driftglobe, a Rival Coin (Tiamat side up), and ~10,700 gp in gems — Morgash's retirement.
- **Reward:** the Thornheart — carried by Invictus.

### 6.3 · The Temple of Shar — ruined temple

*Three scenes from the Tom Cartos **Temple of Night** set — 01 · 02 · 03 Temple of Shar — lit, dressed, and teleporter-linked; full detail in `plans/session-07-the-temple-of-shar.md`. The cult: **Harrow Vane**, high priest (a CR 6 Priest of Shar), with enforcers, zealots and acolytes — all living, all neutral until the break. Harrow carried the **Duskheart** on his person, under his robe — not on the altar, not in the sanctuary. Played in session 7 (8 Sep).*

**The canon of the Temple:**

1. **One murderer, not several.** The man who killed Ann and the four children is **Renlow Veck**, a Daggerford shipping clerk who found that selling names to hags paid better than freight. The murders were **six months** before the party came south (Invictus's backstory); the Sharrans took him **within weeks of them**, which is exactly why the Widows' Ledger entries stop: their supplier was removed. He was their prisoner from then on — half a year in the cells, not more. The gilt-framed merchant portrait out of the hag loot **is him**.
2. **The Sharrans had watched Invictus for years.** They saw the scribe take the Oath of Vengeance and saw what the rage built, and they were genuinely impressed. Their project was not recruitment but **conversion**: make him Shar's — **Dark Justiciar Invictus**, a leader, not a foot soldier.
3. **Shar's real hook is oblivion-as-mercy.** She does not ask anyone to love the dark; she offers to take the memory that hurts, and from inside the pain that *is* kindness. Every cultist in the building took that deal gratefully. They were played as people who were helped.
4. **The Dreamer is an archfey and demigod**, a close ally of Chauntea and Selûne. Shar knows exactly whose heart was in pieces, and keeping her rival's friend **tortured for eternity in grief** is why she would cheerfully pay to see the Heart *spent* rather than returned. Harrow put it plainly: *"As long as the daughter of her allies suffers, she is happy."*
5. **The resurrection-extension offer.** Shar's faithful offered to help Invictus **extend the Heart to resurrect all five** of his dead, past the four beats. The rite is theirs; the price from him was only that the Heart never be made whole — *"Never join it. Never wake the sleeping archfey."* Every beat spent — and any beat stretched — is torment kept flowing. Using the Heart **is** denying the Dreamer, and Shar pays gladly.
6. **He killed Veck, and the conversion took at the table.** First Light *receded* (Lathander withdrew — no wrath, just the dawn leaving the room); the sword became **Midnight** (+2, +1d8 necrotic per hit, Death Armor 1/dusk where Heroism was); and **Shar took his first name** as her price and her mercy. He kept his whole life and every face in it but could not produce the word "Thomas"; he called himself Justiciar. He stayed a PC — Robert kept the sheet — and the party discovered the name-loss themselves. The DM ruled the oath unaffected: no paladin power was lost.

- **The site:** in the forest's far north — a sunless, half-buried temple to **Shar**, lady of loss, night, and forgetting, where her faithful kept the shard and put it to use. Above ground, **the Iris**: a ring of pale stone in an old meadow, four hooded faces watching inward, and at its centre a disc cut in curved petals meeting at a point — a shut stone eye — with stairs cutting down beneath it. They were the one guardian group that knew **exactly** what the shard is: grief made solid, a sorrow that never runs dry — to Shar's people not a curse but a *wellspring*.
- **All living humanoids — no undead** (the Hollow Shrine owns this campaign's undead). The ruling is load-bearing: this is the one ruin where the party killed **people** — mortals with names and reasons, nobody raised or charmed into it, who looked at a stranger's thousand-year mourning and decided to farm it. There were no victims of the shard in this building, only volunteers — which made the Temple the campaign's **moral rehearsal for the finale**, where the enemies were also people, also unmagical, also certain they were right.
- **The temptation of Invictus.** The priest read him before a blade was drawn — vengeance-sworn, Lathander's colors, Oghma's symbol — and opened with a compliment, and it was true: his power comes out of the loneliness of what was taken from him and his thirst to answer it, and they were **impressed**. They recognized a colleague. *You've been making a god's work out of your grief for years.* Come to Shar, who at least does not ask him to forgive.
  - **The offer with the hook in it:** they had **nothing to do with the murders** — and it was true. But they **had the murderer** — one man, Renlow Veck, alive in this temple, taken for reasons of their own — and they would *give* him to Invictus. The vengeance he had organized his life around, disarmed and chained in a room he could walk into that night. The hunt for his family's killer did not end in a name: he met the man.
  - **The proof:** zone of truth was available, never offered, and never needed. Veck confessed unprompted and completely — names sold to hags, a scribe who would not stop pulling threads, a visit meant to frighten, *"I know I killed your family."* The horror of the scene was precisely that everyone was telling the truth.
  - **The shard came only at Shar's price.** Veck was a gift; the Duskheart came with the vow never to join the Heart. Invictus refused the vow — *"we still have a job to do"* — and Harrow: *"then I can't give you the heart."* They died over it.
  - **What they believe:** *a grief that is allowed to finish is spent; a grief kept broken never runs dry.* That is their theology and their business model — and it is, word for word, what the party would have become had they kept the Heart (§7.4). The cult may not have known the village, but **Shar knows the Dreamer** — she knows precisely whose heart this is, which is what made the fifth-beat offer a deliberate cruelty rather than a coincidence.
- **As played:** Mother Wend named Veck from the portrait and her own ledger; a squirrel named Pudgy reported the purple robes at the Iris; Harrow met them at the foot of the stair with Thomas's full name and Ann's. In the cell Morgash grappled Thomas and argued with him — *"He deserves to die, but not here, not now"* — and Thomas broke the hold and drove First Light into Veck's chest. Then he refused Harrow's terms. Harrow *word of recall*ed to the lower level; Invictus bluffed his way past the entrance-floor staff, one of whom bowed to the *Dark Justiciar*; the party cleared the temple floor and fought Harrow to the end, and Jetten's Goldthorn cut him in half. Jetten took the Duskheart from his chest. Morgash cut off Veck's ring finger, signet and all, to carry home as proof.
- **The trace it leaves:** here the grief wasn't warping anything wild — it was being *cultivated*, by people who understood it and chose.
- **Reward:** the Duskheart — carried by Jetten.

### 6.4 · The Widow Fen — drowned bog

*Three scenes from the Tom Cartos **Green Hag Lair** set — 01 Swamp · 02 Treetop · 03 Cavern; full detail in `plans/session-06-the-widow-fen.md`. The coven is **two** hags — **Hazel** (elder, the danger) and **Mabel** (merchant and mimic), both CR 5; their third sister, **Ethel**, was killed near Baldur's Gate by **Lae'zel**. The Tideheart, in Hazel's hand when she died, was the "third sister" keeping their Coven Magic alive. Played in session 6 (31 Aug).*

- **The site:** just north of the valley, where the forest's edge drowns away toward the moors — a fen of black water and drifting mist where a **coven of two hags** nested (three, before Baldur's Gate), and where a shard of pure heartbreak had become an *ingredient*. Hags trade in misery: brew it, wear it, sell it. A bottomless well of it was the best thing to wash into their bog in an age.
- **The hags knew the whole sad story** — Corin, the Dreamer, all of it up to the fog-line (what became of Corin past the fog even they don't know — Appendix A.5) — and would have sold it, cheerfully, mockingly, a real grief rendered down to merchandise. The party reached them at one shard, and the truth came early: the **Sisters' Notes** laid it out — *"The heart is broken in four. RED — the barrow. BLUE — ours, below. GREEN, BLACK — not yet found. The dryad sleeps in the great tree on Greenrest's square. The heart is hers. Whole, it beats four times."* — and the hags confirmed it from their own mouths: she broke her own heart, and her own grief holds her under the tree.
- **They offered partnership:** help them find the green and the black, and when the Heart beats whole, **one beat wakes their sister — the other three are the party's.** Thomas wanted it — the hags claimed to have his family's killer's name, and would trade it only for an unbreakable bond. Jetten refused outright. Hazel called it — *"These aren't going to bargain with us"* — and both hags died in two rounds. Hazel refused surrender: *"Do it. I have nothing to live for."*
- **The trace it left:** the town began to dream that homecoming night for the first time in generations; the next morning Selma heard the first dream report of her life.
- **Reward:** the Tideheart — carried by Gren.

## 7 · The endgame

With all four shards the party could make the heart whole. There was **no final boss** — gathering the Heart didn't wake a monster; it let a thousand-year mourner finally feel her whole loss and go. What stood between the party and the tree was not a creature. It was the village — cornered, terrified, and never once malicious.

### 7.1 · Oswin asks

**By the fourth homecoming, the mayor knew.** He had skipped his tonic since the morning after the second homecoming, dreamed the breaking with the whole town, and worked it out on his own over breakfast: *"She was offering him eternal summer, eternal life, and he refused. It seems we might be the beneficiaries of that."* He called the elders. He knew about the Temple of Shar, and that the body-part trader was dead — he had seen the finger. He never learned the hearts by name. The shape he had right.

The fourth dream took the whole town too (§5.2, A.4), and when the party woke at the town's edge he was already coming — out of breath, in the road near the square, with Pip beside him. The second plea of the morning, asking for the opposite of the first:

> *"I know what you're doing. I saw you in the dream… Please don't do this. We'll do anything. You don't have to let her go. She's been here the whole time. What's the difference?"*

- **He said everything he knew, plainly**, and offered what he had: a key to the city, a home for as long as they wanted it, free meals (Ambrose had already agreed). It was pitiful next to the Broken Heart, and he knew it.
- **There was no threat in him anywhere.** No leverage, no watch. A kind, frightened man asking four armed friends for the life of his town: *"I'm fond of you all and that's what makes this so hard… the town will never prosper again. The fog, I'm sure, will come back."*
- **He was not wrong.** This was the scene's teeth. Greenrest dies the day the Dreamer does — the winters get real, the harvests thin, the mists return. Every word he said was true — and the party had just dreamed the counter-argument in her own voice. Two true things, one road.
- **There was no DC.** Not a Persuasion contest; a conversation between people who like each other and want opposite things.

Invictus answered him: *"It's just evil to keep her trapped there. You know that."* And it was **Pip** who turned him — *"I think he's right, Mayor. They need to do what they need to do."* — and Oswin gave way: *"Oh, Pip, you're right. Very well. I'll tell the guards to stand down. Do what you need to do. I'll go tell the elders."* He kept his word, and the watch never came to the square. The town came anyway — not because the party gathered shards, but because it was afraid. **That is the difference between a village of villains and a village of neighbors, and the finale was built on it.**

### 7.2 · The battle of the square

The finale was a single set-piece in the **Town Square, at the tree**, and the party won it by **holding the square long enough for the heart to go home** — not by killing the town.

- **The heart knot.** At chest height on the trunk, a knot the size of two cupped hands, shaped like a closed heart: warm, and, with a palm laid on it, beating. The Dreamer named it in the fourth dream. **The ruling:** one heart at a time; placing one takes a full turn of concentration; interrupted, start over; absorbed at the placer's next turn.
- **The ritual was a clock, not a check** — five rounds, counted out loud. The **Duskheart** (Jetten) went in first, then the **Graveheart** (Morgash), then the **Tideheart** (Gren), and last the **Thornheart**, pressed in by Invictus leaning from the saddle of his celestial steed: *"This is for you, and blot out all my iniquities, Lathander."*
- **The town itself was the enemy.** Three **Greenrest Mobs** — *The Whole Street*, the *Farmhands*, the *Torchbearers* — with pitchforks, clubs, swords, and thrown cabbages and tomatoes: *"Get them! They can't take our town away! Stop this!"* Nonlethal was melee-only, said before initiative. Gren's *Hypnotic Pattern* froze The Whole Street for the whole fight; Invictus's booming *Thaumaturgy* and Morgash's Intimidation stopped the other two cold for a round; Jetten's *Entangle* pinned the Torchbearers. Invictus put his hands up and refused to attack; Gren swung nonlethal. Oswin had stood the watch down, and Warin never took the field.

### 7.3 · The passing

When the fourth heart was absorbed, the knot opened and the Dreamer stepped out of the trunk holding the whole heart — the tall, blond, wood-and-blossom dryad the party had dreamed all campaign, in the waking world for the first and last time. And in her hands, **the crystal gave.**

> #### THE LINE — the single most important beat in the campaign
>
> Everything the crystal rule bought (§5.1) was spent here, in about fifteen seconds. They had handled the shards for months; they had tried to chip one. As delivered:
>
> > *"On the fourth heart, the knot opens and the dryad steps out of the trunk, holding the whole heart. She says to you, 'It's stone. It has been stone in your hands for so long, warm and hard, and now it beats inside of me again.' She closes her hand around her chest. The light goes out of the stone shards. Their edges lose their shape. It grows soft and warm and wet… It wasn't a jewel at all. Now it's a heart. It's a plain red ordinary meat beating in her hands."*
>
> Nobody explained it. The stone was never the magic; the stone was the part of her that couldn't die.

She took the heart back into her breast, and it became part of her again — it was always her. And she did not rampage. She had already forgiven him in the dream — *he was right*, a life is for spending, and he went to spend his — and forgiven herself; now she was allowed, at last, to follow. The tree threw out one final unbearable bloom, every branch at once, the most alive anything in this valley has ever been — and she was gone. From far off: *"Thank you. Thank you so much. I can never repay you."* (Morgash: *"Yes, you can. Wait."*) No reward, no bargain. **The Broken Heart went with her** — its magic was her, so it died when she did. Nothing was left to carry home but the story.

**The first autumn.** The tree is just a tree now, ordinary, losing its leaves — the first to fall in the square in living memory, and the children have never seen it. The mists rolled slowly back over the valley. The commoners would not speak to the party; the elders understood and could never forgive; Oswin: *"Please, please just go."* And Invictus came to himself and said it: **"Thomas. My name is Thomas."** — Shar's price undone. In the weeks after, the valley went plain: smaller harvests, but real; a few families took the Trade Way south for the winter; Oswin watched the leaves come down from the steps of the Mayor's Tower; and at a sick child's bedside Enid prayed, and something answered (§3.4). They freed a soul a thousand years caged; they also turned off a village's good fortune, in front of the village. That is the whole bittersweet of it — **nobody is wrong enough to make it clean.**

### 7.4 · The dark alternative — four deaths, and whose they are

This was not a footnote, and by the last session it was the loudest argument at the table — louder than the mobs, louder than Oswin. The party could have kept the assembled Heart and never brought it to the tree. Whole, in their hands, it held **four castings of *true resurrection*** — and it kept them precisely because its magic **is** her, still alive, still weeping.

Every number was on the table for weeks before the end, delivered by people the party distrusted:

- **The Widow Fen** — the hags offered partnership: one beat to wake their sister, the other three the party's. The first read on who was tempted: Thomas wanted the deal.
- **The Temple of Shar** — Harrow laid out four true resurrections, stretched by Shar's rite to all five of Thomas's dead, for the price of never joining the Heart; and the Sharrans' theology, that *a grief kept broken never runs dry*. They had just described the valley — and the party, if it kept the Heart.
- **Oswin's plea** — he begged them to leave a soul in its cage so his town could live (§7.1).

On the morning of the Wyrmwood, Invictus argued at breakfast that the dryad might deserve her tree — *"I just don't seem to have my same moral compass anymore"* — and after the dragon he said Midnight had been whispering the answer to him.

**Shar's last offer.** The morning after the Wyrmwood, Jetten's eyes went inky black, and the Duskheart read Shar's last offer aloud through him while Midnight glowed:

> *"Invictus. Killing my high priest was regrettable, but it is forgivable. My offer still stands. Take the heart shards and Midnight to the Dreamer's tree. Find the tree's heart knot and plunge the blade into it. Midnight will drain the power from the tree and the shards. The Dreamer will be trapped. Each shard can grant you a resurrection, and the heart of the tree will grant you a fifth. The shards will be dead, but Midnight will have the ability to give you your five resurrections. You will win. You will get your family back. The town will win. The Dreamer's summer will remain in Greenrest forever. I will win. My will shall be done. Think carefully, Invictus. This is my last offer."*

He wavered — *"What's one fae versus an entire town?"* — and the party talked him back. Jetten: *"It sounds like a trap, Invictus. Like a bargain with the devil."* Morgash: *"It's not the same sword. Don't lie to yourself."* Gren: *"You haven't done anything wrong… you don't need redemption, but if you do this you will need redemption."* He went down to the lakeshore to pray; the fog curled away, and the Dawnfather spoke: *"You are not completely lost, my child, but the sword you wield must be left here. Bury it. First Light is gone."* They buried Midnight there. It still calls to him from the ground.

**And there is no third door.** The castings are not charges in a jewel — they are *her*, the deathlessness she was left holding, handed out one death at a time. Spend one and there is that much less of her to give back; spend four and what remains is a heart with nothing left in it. A party who used the Heart and then brought it to the tree would not have got the passing — they would have got a tree that takes a hollow thing and goes on sleeping. **They could free a thousand-year mourner, or they could have their families back. Not both.**

**What it would have cost:** the tree sickened again; Greenrest kept its summer and never learned what it nearly lost; strangers kept dying in the ruins; and under the square the Dreamer went on exactly as she had for a thousand years — except that the party knew her name, knew what she was, and chose it. Every one of those resurrections drawn out of a woman not allowed to stop crying. It would not have been the bad end. They wouldn't have been cowards, or greedy — only people who wanted someone back, which is precisely what the Dreamer wanted, and what the whole campaign was about from the first dream. A quieter ending, and arguably a crueler one — and the only one in which somebody finally gets what Corin refused. The endings not taken are told in the chronicle's "What If…?" appendix (`book/`).

- **Invictus's oath survived the Temple.** Killing Veck cost him no paladin power — *"plot things can happen, but it's not like you're going to lose your powers."*
- **The heart knot:** one heart at a time; a full turn of concentration to place it; interrupted, start over; absorbed at the placer's next turn (§7.2).

### 9.3 · Player briefing — read-aloud / handout

*Player-facing; only what the party's own research would turn up — none of the real secret. Delivered in-world as the "Broken Heart Briefing" handout.*

You live in **Daggerford**, and through your own digging you've cobbled together a map to the **Broken Heart** — a relic shattered into four shards, scattered in the **Misty Forest**, days south down the Trade Way. A dragon, a hag coven, a cult of Shar, and something prowling a lost shrine each sit on one. There's a single village close enough to base the whole hunt out of — **Greenrest** — so you leave home behind and start the long trek south.

Now the details.

**Daggerford** is a walled market town on the Sword Coast, where the Trade Way fords the River Delimbiyr — caravan dust and ferry crossings, farmland and honest trade, a duke's keep on the hill. The last real comfort before the road turns wild to the south. It's home, and it's where you pieced this together (over drinks at the **Silver Gauntlet**).

There was never one map. There was a bought scrap, a caravanner's story, a line copied from a temple ledger, a favor called in, a rumor chased three towns over. Cobbled together, they all point at the same thing: the **Broken Heart**, shattered long ago into **four shards** and scattered down the wilds in the Misty Forest. Gather the four, and it's yours.

Your digging turned up where they went — and what's sitting on each one now:

- **The Lost Shrine.** A shard fell here and was never recovered; it's lost in the dark below an abandoned shrine. Every account agrees on one detail — something prowls the depths.
- **The Green Dragon's lair.** A young green dragon keeps a shard as the prize of its hoard. It won't part with it kindly.
- **The Temple of Shar.** Worshippers of the Lady of Loss hold a shard and are putting it to some purpose of their own. No one you asked knew what — or cared to talk long.
- **The Hag Coven.** Hags in the moor got hold of a shard and feed their coven magic with it. Bargain at your own risk.

Here's the problem the map lays bare: all four sites are **days south of Daggerford**, clustered in wild country far past the last friendly walls — and there's no living out of a saddle for a job this size. You need a roof, a market, a bed to fall back to between digs. There's exactly one: a village called **Greenrest**, sitting dead-center of the whole spread, the only home base for days in any direction. Little is known about this small village. The Misty Forest has always been the realm of fey and fog, so you don't know what to expect. Regardless, the first move is the long trek south down the Trade Way — leave Daggerford behind, and make Greenrest your staging ground for the foray.

**Before session one — build your character.** Anchor your backstory to at least one of your three pillars: **class, background, and race/lineage.** From those, tell us: how you ended up in **Daggerford**; why *you* are chasing the Broken Heart (coin, glory, a debt, a calling, someone to prove wrong, something to do with one of the four shard locations); and one thread tying you to another party member, or to the road south. Attached are two maps: the world map, and your treasure map.

## A · Appendix — the reveal dreams

*The four reveal dreams of §5.2 — staging, beats, and read-aloud lines, with what was delivered at the table. Each fired on homecoming to Greenrest, in real beds, mid-session — except the fourth, which took the party and the whole town at the town's edge (§5.2).*

**The governing principle — three returns of theater, one of contact.** In the first three dreams the party stood **unseen inside her memory**, watching Corin's story told in order; she was unaware of them, and the dream was immersive theater. Only in the fourth did she **see them and speak to them** — and what she said was a plea: *free me.* The finale weighed **two direct pleas** — hers in the dream, Oswin's in the road (§7.1) — and §7.4's dark alternative meant refusing her to her face. What the dreams never delivered was the release itself; THE LINE and the passing (§7.3) were the finale's payload. And the campaign's northstar (§1) lived in her one repeated sentence: *stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever.*

### A.1 · Dream 1 — the Glimpse (1 shard · session 5, 25 Aug)

- The party stands unseen in her memory: a summer hillside, a **raven-haired** stranger laughing, a feeling of being adored that isn't theirs. **She is unaware of them** — when she drifts near and reaches, it is *through* them, at someone else, and her *"stay this time — stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever"* is spoken to the hillside, not to the room. The sentence played tender at one shard and detonated retroactively when the party heard it pressed in earnest in dreams 2 and 3 (§1, the northstar — the valley is that sentence enacted).
- **As played:** it fired twice. She called them "Corin." On the second night Jetten held out the Graveheart; her eyes widened; the dream cut. The canon reading: the one moment in a thousand years the dream *looked back* — her heart recognizing a piece of itself, not her recognizing them. It foreshadowed A.4's contact.

### A.2 · Dream 2 — the Summer (2 shards · session 7, 8 Sep)

*The party stands inside the happiest memory anyone ever kept, and watches it begin to crack. There is no present-her in this dream — every version of her here is memory, absorbed in him, unaware of her audience.*

- **The meeting.** First and briefly: a blonde woman under the great tree, and Corin — raven-haired, mortal — walking up the hill to her. She looks at him the way the party has felt her look (dream 1's borrowed adoration, given its source).
- **The growing.** A montage, seasons of them, never quite winter. With every pass the valley is *more* — heavier boughs, brighter meadows — because she is blooming it **for him**; it blooms harder every time he smiles (the valley-truth shown, never said). And every pass there is more silver in his hair, and none in hers.
- **The wish.** At the end of it, the two of them under the tree: his silver head against her shoulder, her hand in his hair, neither of them smiling. They both know what's coming, and only one of them can do anything about it — the party has the feeling that he will grow old and die. She turns to him and says it, the sentence the party heard in dream 1, now aimed and meant:

> *"Stay — stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever. Be as I am. Never end."*

- Then morning. The dream ran undressed and ended there.

### A.3 · Dream 3 — the Breaking (3 shards · session 8, 22 Sep)

- Dusk, flowers closing. Played once, straight through — and this time the party was not alone on the hill: the whole town stood with them, hazy figures in the mist. Corin is silver-threaded now, and the offer is not an evening's sweetness — it is an **argument**, and she is losing it. She presses her exact words, desperate, repeating them as if they might work on the third telling:

> *"Stay — stay, and I'll make it summer for you forever. Be as I am. Never end."*

- For one beat the party sees fey-Corin as she imagines him: deathless, beautiful, slightly vacant. The refusal explained without a word of exposition.
- **The refusal, made final** — as delivered:

> *"If you take the ending out of me — if you take away my mortality — there's nothing left that you love. A life isn't for keeping, my dear. It's for spending. Let me go and spend mine."*

- He walks down the hill toward the fog. **She follows exactly as far as she exists** — to where her green meets the mist — and stops, because she ends there. The fog takes him a step at a time.
- **The waiting** *(the strobe — a minute of table time for decades of hers)*: she keeps to the fog-line every dusk, and the seasons wheel past in flashes — real autumns, light winters, brief snow on the orchard, **the last true seasons this valley would wear for a thousand years** — while each spring she makes more glorious than the last: signal-fires, a valley visible from far away, so it can be found again. Decades of it. He never comes. And no ending ever comes back to her — old, or happy, or nothing; she is never told which (A.5).
- **The break, and the burst.** The spring when even she knows a mortal's handful of years is long spent, she breaks — on the not-knowing. She tears her heart out and rips it into four — **red, blue, green, and a blackish purple** — and throws the pieces out in four directions: grief hardening to crystal in the air, the mists blown off the valley in a ring — the valley flinging the fog back to look for him one last time, too late, in every direction — and, where she stood, a woman folding into a tree.
- **The dream ends in the explosion.** No coda, no comfort. They wake with warm stone near their beds and know, all the way down, what they are carrying three quarters of. By breakfast Pip had drawn the four colours in berry juice, and the children had a new game called *The Breaking*.

### A.4 · Dream 4 — the Asking (4 shards · session 9, 29 Sep)

- **Where and when.** Not in a bed: in the late morning, as the party crossed Greenrest's limits, Jetten's moonstone glowed and all four fell into the dream where they stood.
- **Contact.** No hillside this time. The wreck of the memory — the bare hill, the fog close, the tree where she stood — and they were not alone: the whole town was there in their nightclothes, Oswin, Selma, Enid, Ambrose, Warin, Pip, with no mist between them anymore. For the first time in four dreams, she was looking at them.
- **The forgiveness, and the plea** — as delivered:

> *"You've been here before, in the summer, at the fog, at the breaking. You are the ones holding my heart. I want you to know I forgive him. I forgive Corin. I know he chose to leave me. But he was right. I forgive him. And now, after centuries, I can finally forgive myself for holding on so long, for all of it. I'm asking you, begging you, free me. Bring the hearts to my tree. Find the heart knot. Place them in it, one by one, and I will absorb them. On the fourth, I shall be free. Please, help me. I've been trapped for centuries."*

- **As she faded** she looked at Invictus and smiled a little. They woke at the town's edge, the whole town with them — and Oswin was already coming up the road (§7.1).

### A.5 · The open thread — Corin's fate

- **Deliberately unwritten.** Nobody in the fiction ever learned what became of him — not the Dreamer, not the hags (§6.4), not this document. It is the one thread the story left wholly unwritten: a future adventure for anyone who goes looking.
- **The breadcrumb:** the roadside **Ballad Sheet** trinket names Corin — mortal-side memory of him survives in song. Daggerford records, or the temple ledger Gren's research came from, are natural trailheads.
- **The finale needed none of it:** she forgave him in the fourth dream — *he was right* — and was freed without ever learning his fate (§7.3).
- **And if he founded a line somewhere**, a raven-haired descendant is a whole story on its own.
